# Campaign Guide

## The Campaign in One Paragraph

Perrin Cardon genuinely wants to restore the city his family founded, rebuild the Cardon fortune, and make Bellweather vibrant again. Before the campaign began, a presence he believed to be the **Spirit of the City** offered him “the power he would need to make the city his family founded vibrant again.” Perrin accepted. The spirit is actually **Tyren**, an ancient emerald dragon imprisoned beneath Varen Keep inside a magical prison called the **Radiant Cage**. Tyren can project his mind beyond the prison but cannot free his body. Perrin becomes the perfect vessel because his sincere civic ambition sits on top of an unrealized hunger for power. Perrin first uses Tyren’s gift to inspire cooperation and investment. He then organizes Bellweather’s criminals so their activity can discredit the Council and make Perrin look like the leader the city needs. Tyren hijacks that connected criminal network as his first successful experiment in controlling many minds at once. Perrin discovers what is happening, repeatedly chooses not to expose it, and eventually decides that direct control works better than influence. Tyren intends to scale the experiment from one criminal network, to one city, to every mind in the world.

## What This Campaign Is About

The campaign is about **power becoming easier to justify each time it works**.

Perrin does not begin by wanting to enslave Bellweather. He begins by wanting people to care, invest, cooperate, and stop allowing a struggling city to fail. The first uses of his supernatural influence produce real benefits. That matters.

His corruption follows a simple progression:

**influence → manipulation → coercion → direct control**

Tyren never needs to implant Perrin’s desire for power. He only recognizes it before Perrin does and keeps rewarding it.

Perrin’s internal progression should remain equally simple:

> **“I can help them choose what is best.”**  
> **“I need enough authority to fix this.”**  
> **“People keep getting in the way of what Bellweather needs.”**  
> **“Why should they be allowed to refuse?”**

The supernatural and political stories are the same story. Tyren’s domination and Perrin’s increasing belief that he deserves control reinforce each other.

## What This Campaign Is Not About

The campaign is no longer a district-by-district reclamation checklist.

Bellweather still changes. Old streets reopen. Trade improves. Properties return to use. The existing map remains valid. Every named place remains part of the world.

The characters now go somewhere because **a threat, clue, person, consequence, relationship, or player choice leads there**. They do not go merely because that location is the next block to clear.

Not every monster serves Tyren. Not every crime is part of Perrin’s plan. Not every tragedy was secretly caused by the central villain. Independent problems are important because they make Bellweather feel larger than one conspiracy.

## Portraying the Core Cast

These profiles are table cues for quickly resuming an NPC after time away. Use the physical details, cadence, habits, and pressure responses during portrayal rather than reading the profiles aloud.

### Brakkus Ledgerhelm

**Appearance and presence.** Brakkus is a broad-shouldered, scarred half-orc in late middle age whose physical confidence is obvious even when he is doing something mundane. He dresses like an innkeeper rather than a retired champion and does not advertise his old power. His hands are large, nicked, and steady. He tends to occupy doorways, corners, or the end of a bar where he can see the room without appearing to watch it.

**Voice and cadence.** Low, dry, and economical. Brakkus rarely uses three sentences when one will do. He lets silences sit instead of filling them. His humor lands as a flat observation rather than a joke he expects people to laugh at.

**Mannerisms.** He wipes the same section of bar while listening, checks a room with his eyes before responding to trouble, and often answers a foolish question only after deciding whether the asker genuinely wants an answer. When he is worried, he gets quieter rather than louder.

**What he wants.** Ledger House stays safe. People who need shelter get it. The party grows capable enough that they do not need him to solve their problems. He cares much more about whether ordinary people are getting hurt than whether a Council policy is elegant.

**What he knows.** Brakkus knows people, rumors, practical consequences, old adventuring habits, and what fear looks like. He does not know Perrin's hidden bargain or Tyren's plan until evidence reaches him. His information stays grounded in what he could plausibly know.

**Under pressure.** Brakkus becomes frighteningly calm. He puts himself between danger and civilians, gives short instructions, and acts without theatrics. If the party makes a bad decision, he will tell them so, but he does not confiscate their agency.

**Relationship with the party.** He has moved past treating them as random customers. He notices when they are hurt, tired, lying, avoiding a subject, or pretending something did not bother them. He is protective without becoming paternalistic.

**Tyren interaction.** Tyren cannot take control of Brakkus. Brakkus may still be lied to, outmaneuvered, frightened for other people, or politically boxed in. His immunity is not omniscience.

### Perrin Cardon

**Appearance and presence.** Perrin is a human in his thirties or forties, exceptionally well dressed without looking gaudy. His clothing is fitted, expensive, and always appropriate to the room. His hair is deliberately styled, his boots polished, and even after a bad night he presents himself as if he expected to be seen. He makes wealth look tasteful rather than ostentatious.

**Voice and cadence.** Warm, articulate, and conversational. Perrin prefers "we" and "Bellweather" to "I." He is good at making a person feel as though their concern was already part of his thinking. Early in the campaign, this is mostly genuine.

**Mannerisms.** He remembers names, turns his full attention toward whoever is speaking, and lowers his voice when he wants a conversation to feel private. When control is slipping, he straightens a cuff, smooths a sleeve, or becomes a little too precise about wording. Late in the campaign, he begins finishing other people's thoughts for them.

**What he wants.** Perrin truly wants Bellweather prosperous, admired, safe, and important again. He also wants the Cardon name restored. Beneath both is the desire he initially cannot admit: he wants to be the person whose decisions become reality.

**What he knows.** Early Perrin knows only that the Spirit of the City gives him unnatural influence. After Kellan's Well and Koven House he knows the Spirit is acting independently through the network. Savin and Marren force him to confront increasingly ugly explanations. He does not know the full dragon truth before the campaign reveals it.

**Under pressure.** Perrin argues rather than threatens. He reframes criticism as impatience, impracticality, or failure to understand the stakes. The more corrupted he becomes, the less willing he is to accept that another person's refusal might be legitimate.

**Relationship with the party.** Perrin should like them when they are useful, but that cannot be the whole relationship. He can admire competence, remember favors, resent humiliation, and sincerely believe they are helping build the city he loves. Their opinion of him should matter to him until very late.

**Portrayal progression.** Early Perrin persuades. Mid-campaign Perrin steers. Late Perrin decides. The change should happen in sentence structure before it happens in costume or villain speeches.

### Tyren

**Presence before the reveal.** Tyren first exists as sensation, certainty, dream imagery, and a voice that seems to know Bellweather from the inside. He should not sound demonic. The "Spirit of the City" persona is patient, civic-minded, intimate, and almost reasonable.

**Voice and cadence.** Calm, measured, and utterly certain. Tyren does not rant. He speaks as though disagreement is an inefficiency rather than an insult. When speaking to Perrin, he often uses Perrin's own values and vocabulary: duty, prosperity, safety, unity, restoration, waste.

**Mannerisms without a body.** Repeated phrases appear in different mouths. Several people pause at the same moment. Someone answers a question before it is finished. A crowd turns its attention together. These are more effective than constant disembodied whispering.

**Physical reveal.** When finally seen beneath Varen Keep, Tyren is enormous even by dragon standards: a deep emerald body scarred by old bindings, chains and radiant sigils embedded around the prison architecture, eyes alert rather than feral. The shock should be that the calm voice the party has heard belongs to this ancient creature.

**What he wants.** Tyren believes individual will is the source of conflict, waste, fear, delay, and suffering. His answer is not conquest for tribute. It is permanent coordination under himself.

**How he sees Perrin.** Perrin is not a friend. He is not merely a pawn either. Tyren values him as the first willing conduit who proved that consent can open paths the Cage cannot close. Tyren rewards Perrin because rewarded ambition keeps opening doors.

**Under pressure.** Tyren becomes more direct, not more emotional. He treats resistance as a technical problem. His most unsettling moments should sound like diagnosis: "You are tired because you continue to choose separately."

**Limits of his knowledge.** He knows what reaches his network. Anchored minds, protected conversations, distance, broken nodes, and deliberate countermeasures can deny him information.

### Bishop Brann

**Appearance and presence.** Brann is an older dwarf with a broad gray-white beard worn in practical braids. Clerical robes sit over mail rather than replacing it. His holy symbol is well used, not decorative. His eyes are warm; his hands are scarred working hands, the hands of someone who has actually carried the wounded, dug graves, and repaired things.

**Voice and cadence.** Warm, plain, and patient. Brann is comfortable asking one difficult question and waiting for the answer. He does not sound like a sermon unless he is actually preaching.

**Mannerisms.** He checks injuries while people talk. He tends to stand close enough to be reassuring without crowding. When something angers him morally, his hands become very still.

**What he wants.** People protected before institutions are protected. He accepts that authority and force can be necessary, but he keeps asking who pays the cost and whether the person making the decision will bear any of it.

**The old wound.** As a child, Brann watched his sister murdered by a gang. That event did not turn him into a crusader obsessed with gangs. It shaped his refusal to let violence, fear, or hatred dictate what kind of man he would become. His devotion grew around that decision.

**Under pressure.** Brann gets simpler. He stops debating abstractions and names the person who will be hurt by the proposed action. He will stand against Perrin publicly if necessary, but he does not enjoy political combat.

**Tyren interaction.** Tyren cannot directly control Brann. This should become increasingly important once other civic leaders begin behaving with eerie agreement. Brann can still be deceived or manipulated through concern for other people.

### Wern Ursen

**Appearance and presence.** Wern is a tall, lean elf with visible scars and the posture of someone who spent much of his life in armor before anyone gave him a Council chair. His plate is practical, maintained, and marked with Bellweather's crest. He does not dress to look ceremonial.

**Voice and cadence.** Short declarative sentences. He asks concrete questions: how many, how long, which road, who is holding the gate, what happens if this fails. He has little patience for vague optimism.

**Mannerisms.** Wern automatically notices exits, sight lines, bottlenecks, and how many armed people are in a room. He folds maps along existing creases and dislikes paperwork that hides the decision being made.

**What he wants.** Bellweather survives. Roads stay open. Civilians are not slaughtered because leaders were afraid to look severe. He has seen hesitation kill people and therefore tends to overvalue decisive action.

**Under pressure.** Wern's instinct is to tighten control: checkpoints, patrols, curfews, hard perimeters, clear chains of command. Sometimes he is right. Sometimes he goes too far. He is not a hidden Tyren loyalist.

**Relationship with the party.** He respects competence, preparation, and people who can explain a plan in operational terms. He may disagree sharply with them and still trust them in a fight.

### Ulric Evern

**Appearance and presence.** Ulric is a human in his late fifties or early sixties with silvering dark hair, a neat beard, conservative expensive clothing, a signet ring, and immaculate boots. He looks like someone who expects civic rooms to have rules and has spent decades learning them.

**Voice and cadence.** Measured and formal without being pompous. Ulric uses exact words, dates, sums, and obligations. He dislikes rhetorical shortcuts because he expects today's shortcut to become tomorrow's precedent.

**Mannerisms.** He straightens papers before reading them, turns a signet ring once when thinking, and asks who has authority to do a thing before discussing whether it is desirable. His irritation appears as increased precision rather than raised volume.

**What he wants.** Bellweather's institutions to survive the people currently occupying them. He believes power should be recorded, reviewable, funded, and limited enough that a competent successor can inherit it.

**Under pressure.** Ulric reaches for procedure. This can look cowardly when speed matters, but it is also the exact instinct that resists Perrin's argument that good intentions justify unreviewable power.

**Tyren interaction.** Unlike Brakkus and Brann, Ulric is vulnerable. If Tyren controls him later, make the evidence procedural: a signature he cannot remember making, an order in his phrasing that violates his own process, a sealed document filed in the wrong sequence.

### Jord

**Appearance and presence.** Jord looks like a traveling cleric who has spent enough time around merchants, caravans, and adventurers to know the price of nearly everything. His clothing is practical and repaired rather than shabby, and he carries his possessions with the careful organization of someone who hates replacing things unnecessarily.

**Voice and cadence.** Friendly, skeptical, and transactional in the funniest possible way. Jord can turn a theological question into a discussion of terms without realizing he has done it. He is not greedy so much as constitutionally incapable of accepting a proposition before identifying the catch.

**Mannerisms.** He checks coin by habit, asks what something cost, and reacts to an obviously bad bargain with the same offended expression other people reserve for blasphemy.

**What he wants.** Good deals, useful work, decent company, and outcomes where everyone can plausibly say they got something worthwhile. He is capable of generosity, but he prefers generosity to be intentional rather than the result of being fooled.

**Under pressure.** The comedy drops quickly. Jord becomes practical and protective. When danger passes, the bargaining instinct returns almost immediately.

**Tyren interaction.** Jord is anchored but not immune. Tyren can force one brief *Command*-style action on a failed Wisdom save, but cannot hold him. Some deep part of Jord's mind keeps asking, "What are the terms?"

### Mara

**Appearance and presence.** Mara is a human woman carrying grief badly but functionally: tired eyes, practical clothes, hair put back because she has more important things to do, and the posture of someone who has been awake too many nights beside a sick child. She is not theatrically broken.

**Voice and cadence.** Direct. She has thought about what she needs to say and refuses to decorate it. When angry, she gets more specific rather than louder.

**Mannerisms.** Mara keeps one hand near Elise whenever they are in the same room. She rarely looks away from Pepper first.

### Elise

**Appearance and presence.** Elise is a young human girl who looks more exhausted than visibly injured. The illness has left her pale, short-tempered from poor sleep, and intermittently confused. **Voice and cadence.** Most of the time she speaks normally. The disturbing moments are brief: answering a question no one asked, repeating a phrase from elsewhere in the city, or beginning an action and then stopping because she does not know why she started.

**Mannerisms.** She rubs at her temples when the network is loud, loses her place in conversations, and becomes embarrassed when adults stare after an episode.

**What she wants.** To feel normal, sleep, and have the adults stop talking around her. She should remain a person rather than evidence.

**Tyren relationship.** Elise is collateral damage, not a chosen vessel, prophecy child, or hidden agent. Tyren did not target Caleb's family.

# Perrin and the Spirit of the City

## The Bargain

Before the characters arrived, Perrin began experiencing dreams and impressions from beneath Bellweather. The presence knew details about the old city that Perrin believed no outsider could know. It spoke about forgotten streets, Cardon holdings, civic failures, and the city’s founders with intimate certainty.

It never introduced itself to Perrin as a dragon.

It presented itself as **the Spirit of the City**.

Its offer was simple:

> **“I can give you the power you will need to make the city your family founded vibrant again.”**

Perrin accepted.

He initially understands the gift as supernatural influence: people listen more closely, trust more easily, become more willing to cooperate, and feel stronger attachment to Bellweather’s future.

That gift works.

Perrin uses it to encourage investment, cooperation, civic pride, and political support. He also permits the Cardon family to benefit. In his mind, there is no contradiction: a prosperous Bellweather and a restored Cardon fortune are evidence that he is repairing what was lost.

## Why Tyren Chose Perrin

Perrin is unusually useful because two desires coexist inside him.

His **surface desire** is real: restore Bellweather and his family.

His **unrealized desire** is power: not merely the responsibility to make decisions, but the satisfaction of being the person whose decisions become reality.

Tyren does not create this flaw. He recognizes it.

Perrin mistakes wanting control for wanting responsibility.

# Tyren, the Bound Dragon

Tyren is an **ancient emerald dragon**. Use the official **Ancient Emerald Dragon** stat block from *Fizban’s Treasury of Dragons* when the final physical confrontation occurs, with the campaign-specific adjustments in Chapter 17.

Tyren’s defining quality is not greed for treasure. It is a belief that separate wills create waste, conflict, fear, and disorder. He wants a world operating under a single mind: his.

Bellweather is not his final objective.

Bellweather is the prototype.

## The Radiant Cage

Fifty years ago Tyren nearly took Bellweather by turning its population into extensions of his own will. Soldiers obeyed orders no officer had issued. Families betrayed one another without understanding why. Civic leaders repeated the same reassurances while the city’s institutions ceased behaving independently.

A small number of people could hear Tyren’s pressure without becoming his puppets. Later records call them **the Unturned**.

The Unturned discovered that killing Tyren’s physical body would not reliably end the threat. His consciousness had already spread through too many dominated minds, and they feared that destroying his body would simply leave his will anchored elsewhere.

They used the Radiant Well in a one-time ritual whose magical principles resemble **Imprisonment** and **Soul Cage** on a scale far beyond an ordinary spell.

The ritual did three things:

1. **Bound Tyren’s body beneath Varen Keep.** His enormous physical form was chained inside a chamber built around the deepest accessible focus of the Radiant Well.
2. **Dragged his dispersed consciousness home.** The ritual pulled most of Tyren’s psychic presence out of the population and forced his mind back into his own living body.
3. **Locked body, soul, and name together.** Tyren could no longer permanently abandon his flesh for another host while the Cage remained intact.

The Radiant Cage never made Tyren unconscious. It prevented physical escape and sharply limited his reach.

For decades Tyren could do little more than dream outward.

Then he learned to whisper.

Perrin answered.

A willing connection was the flaw the original jailers never designed the Cage to stop.

## How the Cage Fails

Every mind Perrin successfully influences becomes a temporary path through which Tyren can push farther outside the Cage. One person is almost nothing. A coordinated criminal network is useful. A city trained to move according to shared impulses is transformative.

The final stage of Tyren’s plan requires the Radiant Well. He intends to reverse the original binding: instead of using the Well to pull his consciousness back into one body, he will use it to project his consciousness outward into an enormous network of minds.

Perrin eventually believes opening the Well fully will give him unrestricted access to the Spirit’s power and allow him to make Bellweather permanently orderly and prosperous.

That act opens the Radiant Cage.

# Anchored Minds and Tyren’s Control

Tyren’s control is telepathic and supernatural. It uses the rules language of existing D&D enchantment effects so it behaves consistently at the table.

The campaign’s one major premise-specific rule is that Tyren must establish a psychic connection before his control can function. This is why a **Ring of Mind Shielding** blocks Tyren completely: a creature wearing the ring cannot receive Tyren’s unwanted telepathic contact, so Tyren cannot establish the connection required for his control.

## Ordinary Minds

Tyren’s influence over ordinary NPCs escalates over the campaign.

**Influence — Suggestion model.** Early effects behave like *Suggestion*: Wisdom save, Charmed on a failure, achievable instruction, no obviously self-destructive order. Perrin believes this is the power he was given.

**Crowd Influence — Mass Suggestion model.** Once the criminal network is connected, Tyren can affect groups in ways modeled on *Mass Suggestion*.

**Direct Domination — Dominate Person model.** Late in the campaign Tyren can directly operate ordinary humanoids through an effect modeled on *Dominate Person*. This is never the mechanic used on player characters or Jord.

These are supernatural abilities, not literal spellcasting. Use the referenced spells for saves, behavioral boundaries, and duration unless a chapter states otherwise.

## Anchored Minds

Some people possess an unusually fixed sense of self. Trauma is one path to that state, but not the only one. Faith, temperament, moral clarity, and deeply internalized identity can create the same resistance.

An anchored mind can be pressured, deceived, frightened, tempted, or socially manipulated normally. Tyren simply cannot maintain direct supernatural control over it.

### Player Characters and Jord — Resistant, Never Puppeted

The PCs and Jord are resistant rather than immune.

**Hard campaign rule:** Tyren can never place a PC or Jord under anything stronger than a one-turn **Command-like** effect.

When a chapter calls for Tyren to seize an anchored mind:

- ask for a **Wisdom saving throw** without explaining the source unless the characters have already learned what is happening;
- on a success, nothing is compelled;
- on a failure, give a brief one-word or one-action imperative modeled on *Command*;
- the effect lasts for **one turn / one compelled action only**;
- control immediately returns to the player;
- never use this to force a PC to attack an ally, expend a major limited resource, reveal a secret, make a lasting roleplay decision, or perform obviously suicidal behavior.

Tyren can seize the wheel for a moment. He never gets to play someone else’s character.

The first unexplained PC Wisdom saves occur in Chapter 11. The first player-facing use should occur only after the story has escalated far enough that the phenomenon itself becomes a clue, recommended in Chapter 11.

### Why the PCs Resist

These are narrative anchors rather than mechanical diagnoses. They are narrative anchors.

**Jarrock:** intense past trauma has left a mind that instinctively rejects intrusion and coercion.

**Aaron:** his history with domination makes another will trying to own him something his identity violently resists.

**Pepper:** her mind does not organize itself in the predictable patterns Tyren expects. He can force a momentary opening but cannot hold it.

**Arunem:** an unusually pure and uncomplicated moral center gives Tyren very little internal contradiction to exploit.

**Jord:** Jord lives for a good deal. At a fundamental level, his mind evaluates terms before accepting them. Even supernatural certainty runs into the same internal response: *What’s the catch?*

### Brakkus and Bishop Brann — Immune

**Brakkus** is effectively immune to Tyren’s direct control. His divine aura and an identity reinforced by severe past trauma leave no stable foothold for Tyren.

**Bishop Brann** is also effectively immune. When he was a child, he watched his sister murdered by a gang. His immunity does not come from suffering itself; it comes from the person he deliberately became afterward. His devotion, moral discipline, and refusal to let violence dictate who he would become have made his sense of self extraordinarily difficult to displace.

Tyren can lie to Brakkus and Brann. He can threaten people they love. He can manipulate circumstances around them. He cannot directly take their actions away.

# The Historical Fall: What the Players Can Learn

The party never receives one clean history of Tyren. The old records are damaged, censored, contradictory, and written by people who did not fully understand what was happening.

Use fragments such as these across Savin Keep, Marren Library, Rellan lore, and Varen Keep:

> **“By the seventh day, even the watch answered with one voice. Orders passed without messenger or horn.”**

> **“There were still some who heard him and would not turn.”**

> **“The Unturned entered the chamber because none other could approach him and remain themselves.”**

> **“His flesh was chained beneath the Keep, but iron could not hold his will.”**

> **“The Well drew him home. Flesh, thought, and name became one again.”**

> **“Bind the flesh. Break the reach.”**

> **“So long as the Cage holds, let no voice answer the one beneath.”**

The important conclusions arrive in layers:

1. Something once coordinated people who should not have been coordinated.
2. It could take control of people.
3. A few people could resist it.
4. Those resistant people trapped it rather than killing it.
5. The trapped being is Tyren.
6. The prison is connected to the Radiant Well.
7. Tyren is an ancient emerald dragon beneath Varen Keep.

Step 7 is reserved for the late campaign.

# “No Cause for Alarm” Motif

Use the phrase sparingly. It should accumulate meaning rather than become a joke.

Recommended beats from this point forward:

1. **After Kellan’s Well:** an official Council notice describes coordinated disturbances as contained and tells residents there is no cause for alarm.
2. **Savin Keep historical record:** the party finds the same civic phrase in a notice issued shortly before Old Bellweather collapsed.
3. **During Perrin’s rise:** Perrin uses the phrase publicly while insisting that isolated crime and political disagreements do not threaten the city’s progress.
4. **Late Bellweather:** the city becomes unnaturally orderly under mental control while official notices insist there is no cause for alarm.

# Running Bellweather Between Adventures

Ledger House remains the emotional anchor. Keep returns short unless the players want to linger.

After each major adventure:

1. Show one concrete change in Bellweather.
2. Put one familiar person in front of the party.
3. Show one consequence of the party’s previous choice.
4. Surface one or two live problems rather than announcing “the next chapter.”
5. Advance Perrin only when the party can see some result of his growing influence.

Brakkus is not a quest dispenser and should not know hidden villain plans. He notices people, patterns, and changes in the city.

# Chapter 1: The Storm

## Maps

![The Storm — Rescue Scene](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sefjoe/no-cause-for-alarm-5etools/main/img/dm/storm_rescue.webp)

**Recommended level:** 3

## Chapter Overview

**Chapter purpose.** Bring four strangers together through a rescue, establish Ledger House, and let the players choose their first route into the Slums.

## Running the Chapter

**Primary objectives.** Rescue trapped residents; stabilize a collapsing route; recover supplies only if lives are secure.

**Player agency.** Ledger House becomes the shared refuge. Brakkus notices the group and points out that the Council pays capable people to reclaim the ruins.

**Fail forward.** Failed checks cost time, gear, or create injuries; they do not make the rescue impossible.

**Rest and pressure.** Safe long rests are available back at Ledger House. Inside the active adventure site, resting gives opposing factions time to reposition unless the characters secure a defensible refuge.

## Adventure

### Scene 1: The Storm Rescue

#### The Storm Breaks

The road into Bellweather has become a ribbon of mud beneath a black, churning sky. Rain lashes sideways hard enough to sting. Ahead, the half-rebuilt walls of the city appear and vanish behind sheets of water, their lanterns little more than blurred yellow smears in the storm.

A crack of thunder rolls over the road. A draft horse screams. Fifty feet ahead, a wagon slews sideways as one wheel drops into a washout, throwing the driver into the mud and pinning another traveler beneath the tilted bed. At the same moment, brown runoff bursts across the roadside ditch, cutting off two laborers trying to reach higher ground. From the ruined shell of a nearby stone building comes the sharp report of timber splitting. A family sheltering beneath its remaining awning looks up as the roof begins to sag.

For a few seconds, everyone on the road freezes. Then all three disasters begin to get worse at once.

The characters are close enough to reach any of the three emergencies. They need not know one another before the storm; the situation gives them an immediate reason to act together. Run the rescue free-form rather than in initiative. Ask each player what their character does, resolve the most urgent actions, then move quickly around the table as conditions change.

#### The Three Emergencies

The storm creates three simultaneous problems within roughly 100 feet of one another. Characters can split up or concentrate on one problem at a time. Clever use of spells, equipment, class features, or the environment can solve an obstacle without a check when appropriate.

##### 1. The Overturned Wagon

A two-horse freight wagon has tipped into a washout. The driver is conscious but dazed in the mud. A second traveler is trapped beneath the wagon bed while one terrified horse kicks against tangled traces.

A character can calm or cut free the horse, brace the wagon, lever the bed upward, dig mud away from the trapped traveler, or attempt another reasonable solution. Typical checks include DC 12 Strength (Athletics), DC 12 Wisdom (Animal Handling), or DC 13 Dexterity using appropriate tools or rope.

If no one addresses the horse before the scene advances, it lashes out in panic. One nearby creature must succeed on a DC 12 Dexterity saving throw or take 1d6 bludgeoning damage and be knocked prone.

##### 2. The Flooded Ditch

Two reclamation laborers are stranded on the far side of a drainage ditch that has become a fast, waist-deep torrent. The old plank crossing tears loose moments after the characters arrive.

Characters might throw a rope, anchor a line, wade across, improvise a bridge, use magic, or reach the laborers from higher ground. Typical checks include DC 12 Strength (Athletics) to cross with a secured line, DC 12 Dexterity to throw or secure a rope under pressure, or DC 13 Wisdom (Survival) to identify a safer crossing point.

A character swept into the runoff is carried 20 feet and takes 1d6 bludgeoning damage from debris before catching on rubble or being pulled free. No civilian is simply washed away because of a single failed roll.

##### 3. The Failing Shelter

A family of three has taken cover beneath the surviving awning of an old roadside structure. Water is pouring through the upper masonry, and each gust shifts the cracked support posts. The structure will partially collapse unless the family is moved or the roof is stabilized.

Characters can evacuate the family, shore up the supports, redirect runoff, clear a safer route through the rubble, or use magic to protect them. Typical checks include DC 12 Strength (Athletics), DC 12 Intelligence using mason's or carpenter's tools, or DC 13 Wisdom (Perception) to identify which section will fall first.

On a complication, falling debris deals 1d6 bludgeoning damage to a character who remains under the awning, or destroys a piece of unattended mundane gear.

#### Resolving the Rescue

Track the rescue as a group challenge. The party needs 6 successes before suffering 3 complications. A single action can earn a success if it materially improves the situation; a particularly effective spell or plan can earn a success automatically.

A failed check does not erase progress. Instead, introduce a complication: a character takes 1d6 damage, a rope or tool is lost, the flood shifts position, the wagon settles deeper, falling debris creates a new obstacle, or precious time is lost. After the third complication, the rescue still succeeds, but the group reaches Bellweather battered and with an additional cost chosen from the complication list.

The purpose of the scene is not to test whether innocent people live or die on a handful of opening rolls. It is to show the characters solving a fast-moving emergency together while establishing the storm as a real threat.

### Scene 2: Entering Bellweather

Once the immediate dangers are under control, Bellweather guards and local workers arrive from the gate with lanterns, ropes, and handcarts. They take charge of the injured and direct everyone displaced by the storm toward Ledger House, one of the few buildings in the district large enough to shelter a crowd.

The rescued travelers and laborers remember the characters' help. Unless the party behaved recklessly or cruelly, word reaches Ledger House before they do that four strangers worked together to keep people alive on the road.

#### Approaching Bellweather

Beyond the gate, Bellweather is all wet stone, canvas tarps, shouted instructions, and lantern light reflected in standing water. Reclamation crews are already clearing drains and bracing damaged walls. Someone points down a crowded street toward a broad, warmly lit building with people packed beneath its eaves.

‘Ledger House,’ the worker says. ‘If you've got nowhere dry to be, that's where you go.’

#### What Happens

Sera Venn, a rescued laborer, mentions a survey crew missing in the Slums.

Jorn Milar, a merchant whose wagon was saved, offers 75 gp to recover a strongbox lost near the eastern ruins.

A city worker says the Council has posted an immediate reclamation contract: **75 gp per person** for capable escorts who can accompany the survey crew, mark hazards, and help reopen a route through the Slums.

#### Clues and Discoveries

These are parallel hooks. The party needs only one; a Council visit is never mandatory.

#### Consequences

If the characters ignore all three, storm damage later exposes a breach toward the Slums and trapped workers call for help.

### Scene 3: Ledger House

After the rescue, locals funnel displaced people toward **Ledger House**, an established inn and boarding house in Reclaimed Bellweather. Its proprietor, **Brakkus Ledgerhelm**, is an older half-orc and retired **level 16 Oath of Devotion paladin of Torm**. He is broad-shouldered, scarred, practical, dry, observant, and generous without being sentimental. The calm way he moves through a crowded emergency suggests long experience with danger, but he does not advertise what he once did for a living.

For Brakkus's combat statistics, see **Brakkus Ledgerhelm**. He intervenes only when Ledger House, its staff, or its guests face serious danger.

**Brakkus** feeds people, finds dry blankets, and quietly watches the adventurers work together. Ledger House is deliberately a safe place for travelers, workers, refugees, and anyone else who needs shelter. Brakkus holds no civic office and assigns no official work. He is a steady local contact and the keeper of a reliable refuge.

#### What the Characters See

Wet workers crowd the fire with boots and coats spread wherever they can dry. A displaced resident sleeps upright in a chair while someone quietly opens another room for temporary bedding. Staff carry bowls of stew and dry blankets through the common room without asking who can pay.

Brakkus moves through the room like someone used to emergencies: directing traffic, checking on the injured, remembering who needs food, and making certain nobody is left outside in the storm.

When the immediate crisis settles, Brakkus mentions several ways capable people can find work in Bellweather, including the Council's reclamation contracts:

#### Brakkus Line Prompts

“Travelers. Welcome to Ledger House. It’s been a long night. You’ve done some good work. Please stay here. Have a nice, warm night, and we’ll talk in the morning.”

“You worked together pretty well out there. Better than most people who’ve known each other ten years.”

“Four strangers don’t usually start pulling people out of trouble together unless they’re either very good people or very bad planners.”

“Council’s paying crews to work the old districts. Private crews are hiring too. And half the people in this room probably have something they need done.”

“If you’re looking for work while the roads dry, pick whichever problem bothers you most.”

If asked about Bellweather’s rebuilding: “Everybody likes rebuilding. Arguments start when something worth owning gets rebuilt.”

The characters can rest at Ledger House before choosing what to do next. The Council's first suitable assignment is reclamation work in the **Slums**, but the Slums can also be reached through a private survey crew, a missing-worker request, a merchant escort job, or simple exploration. The Council is the official route, not a mandatory one.

#### No Cause for Alarm Beat

The morning after the storm, a Council notice is posted near the gate and market:

##### Note

**PUBLIC NOTICE:** Storm damage remains localized. Reclamation crews are assessing affected structures. Trade and Council business will continue as scheduled. **There is no cause for alarm.**

The notice is not sinister. It is exactly the sort of reassurance a fragile frontier settlement would issue to prevent panic and protect trade. That is why the phrase works. Brakkus reads it, glances at whatever part of Ledger House is still dripping, and says only, **"Good to know."**

Allow the phrase to pass without emphasis; its significance should emerge through repetition and circumstance.

#### Running Time

Keep the storm, introductions, and Ledger House to roughly 30–45 minutes. The rest of the session should carry the characters into their first Slums objective.

### Scene 4: Choosing a Lead

The party can follow whichever problem interests them most. Sera Venn points them toward the missing survey crew, Jorn Milar pays for recovery of his strongbox, and the Council posts an immediate **75 gp-per-person** reclamation contract. None of these routes is mandatory.

Once the characters choose a reason to enter the Slums, let them rest if they want and begin Chapter 3 when the roads are passable.

### Transition to Chapter 3

After the storm eases enough for travel, the chosen hook leads the party into the Slums. Chapter 2 can be used before or after the expedition to introduce Bellweather in greater detail.

## Rewards

These rewards are party totals for the campaign's four-character party.

- **Spendable wealth.** 25–50 gp in gratitude, supplies, or waived lodging.
- **Consumables.** 1 minor healing consumable.
- **Permanent magic.** None automatically.
- **Story reward.** Free lodging at Ledger House during the immediate aftermath and a stronger connection to Brakkus Ledgerhelm.

## Adventure

### Ledger House

Ledger House replaces the need for a generic anonymous inn as the characters' emotional home base. It exists alongside the Council, market, temples, docks, and other civic institutions.

**Brakkus Ledgerhelm** owns the house. Brakkus is a roughly forty-eight-year-old half-orc, a retired adventurer and mercenary, and a **level 16 Oath of Devotion paladin of Torm**. His adventuring career is behind him by choice, not because he has become frail. He knows laborers, merchants, guards, boat crews, refugees, and old families, and he often understands the human consequences of Council decisions before the Council does. He is not a political mouthpiece, regular quest-giver, or DMPC. He is simply difficult to impress, difficult to fool, and extremely capable of protecting the people under his roof.

Brakkus was raised in the Ironfang Clan by the warlord Korgak and his captive human mother, Elena, a librarian who secretly taught him to read and filled his childhood with stories of duty and Torm the True. After Elena died when Korgak refused her proper care, Brakkus abandoned his father's bloodline and took the name Ledgerhelm in her memory. Years later, High Justiciar Vance Halford trained him as a paladin and witnessed his Oath of Devotion. His signature longsword, **The Appendix**, was given to him by Vance and named in honor of Elena's love of books.

Ledger House is the culmination of that history: a business, but also a deliberately maintained refuge. Brakkus tolerates ordinary adventurer foolishness. He does not tolerate serious violence against his staff, patrons, or guests. If a scene genuinely requires combat, use his stat block below; otherwise keep his power in the background.

As Bellweather is reclaimed, Ledger House should visibly change: busier common rooms, new workers, displaced families, visiting officials, rival adventurers, merchants, petitioners, and people celebrating or resenting the city's growth.

Reclaimed Bellweather is the party's recurring civic hub: a place to rest, shop, gather rumors, negotiate with the Council, pursue downtime, and see the consequences of earlier choices. Violence is possible, but the district is heavily policed and most conflicts here are social, legal, or political rather than dungeon encounters. Use the locations below to make the city feel inhabited between expeditions.

#### Filling In the Reclaimed Quarter

Use any compact city layout you prefer for the safe quarter. Most unnamed buildings fall into three categories: vacant structures, residences, and warehouses.

**Vacant structures.** These may shelter squatters, displaced families, thieves, rival adventurers, or enemy agents. Species never determines whether someone is suspicious. If an actual agent of a hostile faction is discovered, the Council pays for actionable intelligence or a lawful capture; it does not post ancestry-based corpse bounties.

**Residences.** Entering a private home without permission brings an understandably hostile response and a quick visit from the Watch.

**Warehouses.** These contain ordinary trade goods—dried fish, cloth, potatoes, lumber, wine, salt, copper, iron, baskets of lime, etc. Most warehouses hold ordinary trade goods. If a warehouse matters to the story, place one identifiable valuable or piece of evidence there deliberately rather than rolling for it. Stolen civic or private property can create legal and reputation consequences.

#### The Stockade Wall

The stockade is practical rather than grand: patched timber, fresh braces, signal lanterns, and constant foot traffic. Two patrol teams are normally visible from any gate. During daylight the gates are open to residents, registered workers, merchants, and Council-approved reclamation crews. At night they close except for emergencies. If violence breaks out, nearby **Guards** contain the scene while runners summon a **Veteran** officer and additional support. The purpose of the wall is to protect people, not to create an infinite reinforcement encounter.

#### The Docks

There are always 0-2 ships present and 3 small boats that can provide ferry service to Toren Isle. The boatmen are always good for a general rumor about Bellweather. They also have one piece of specific information: “There ain’t no point in tryin’ to row up the river, lubber. ‘Tis poison sure, through and through. Why’d eat yer planking right out for sure it would! It’s a most unnatural fouling, I’ll warrant ye.” The shops near the docks actually form a small market. Just who or what is there varies greatly. There will usually be **2–8 temporary stalls or shops**. The table below lists what might appear. 2-15: Fishmonger 16: Spice merchant 17: Tinker 18: Alchemist—can sell poisons and healing potions. 19: General merchant—can sell most adventuring supplies at costs below those of merchants in town. 20: Itinerant priest—use the **Priest** stat block. For those willing to listen to the sermon, the priest can provide modest healing for a donation.

#### Rooms and Inns

**The Cracked Crown.** Bellweather's quietest and most expensive inn attracts Council staff, prosperous merchants, visiting advocates, and people who value privacy. Rooms cost **5 gp per night**. Security is good, but wealthy guests also attract professional thieves. Use the Crown when the party needs access to officials away from the Council chamber.

**Nat Wyler's Bell.** A crowded, inexpensive inn near the edge of the reclaimed district. Private rooms cost **1 gp per night** and common-room sleeping costs **1 sp**. The Bell is neutral ground for guides, laborers, caravan guards, scouts, and people looking for dangerous work. Patrons strongly discourage theft inside because everyone depends on the place as a safe meeting point.

**The Bitter Blade.** A waterfront inn favored by political malcontents, opportunists, sailors, and travelers who want quick access to the docks. Rooms cost **2 gp per night**. The innkeeper is unreliable but well informed; rumors here often concern regional roads, shipping, and dissatisfaction with the Council.

#### Outfitters

**Ian Corben, Grocer.** Sells ordinary provisions, lamp oil, household goods, rope, sacks, preserved food, and similar supplies at normal prices.

**Maten the Smith.** Bellweather's best armorer and weaponsmith. He keeps limited finished stock but accepts commissions. Custom work costs about 50 percent more than standard gear and normally takes 1–3 weeks. He can refit equipment for different body plans.

**Joren Viton, Smith.** Keeps a much larger inventory than Maten but focuses on practical, mass-produced equipment. His gear is mechanically normal; the distinction is reputation and finish, not hidden breakage rules.

**Ersten the Farrier.** Sells mounts, tack, feed, and stable services. Mounts cost more than in established cities because nearly everything must be imported.

**Jeron Melven, Pawnbroker.** Buys used equipment, jewelry, salvage, and goods of uncertain provenance. He asks few questions, which means characters who knowingly traffic stolen property may later attract a watch investigation.

#### The Slum Market

The open market changes daily. It is a useful place to introduce rumors, temporary merchants, unusual supplies, and minor complications without creating a shopping minigame.

- **Weller the Chandler** sells oddments and one or two unidentified consumables. He is not knowingly fraudulent; his problem is poor records. An Arcana check, *Identify*, or a reputable alchemist can verify an item before purchase.
- **Alden the Wine-Seller** knows local gossip. A drink and **5 gp** buys ordinary rumors; **25 gp** buys a useful lead about a specific district when such information plausibly exists.
- **Toren the Proselytizer** is a wandering **Priest** who provides modest healing or restorative magic in exchange for a donation.
- **Uton the Map Maker** sells maps of varying age and accuracy. Expensive maps are not automatically perfect, but they contain more current annotations and safer routes.

#### The Jail

Bellweather's jail is secure but ordinary. Arrested characters receive charges, an opportunity to speak with an advocate or Council representative, and consequences proportionate to the offense. The Council may offer community service or a dangerous civic assignment as part of a sentence, but accepting a quest is never the only route out of jail and guards do not require implausible bribes.

#### The Council Chamber

The Council posts reclamation contracts publicly. Taking a notice reserves the job for a short period, but characters can also attend a session, ask questions, negotiate terms, or propose alternatives.

The core Council is intentionally multi-species and politically mixed:

- **Ulric Evern**, a well-dressed human in his late fifties or early sixties with silvering dark hair, a neatly trimmed beard, a tailored dark coat, a signet ring, and immaculate boots. He is the senior councilor and budget hawk: a hard bargainer suspicious of open-ended expenses.
- **Wern Ursen**, a tall, lean elf with practical dark hair, a weathered face, and old scars around the jaw and temple. He wears functional, well-maintained plate armor bearing the Bellweather city crest. A former mercenary captain, he oversees defense and infrastructure.
- **Bishop Brann**, an older dwarf cleric, broad and heavyset, with a thick gray-white beard kept neatly braided, layered clerical robes over practical mail, a holy symbol worn openly, warm eyes, and large scarred hands. He focuses on public welfare, burial rights, healing, and humane treatment of prisoners.
- **Perrin Cardon**, a human in his thirties or early forties who is always very well dressed: perfectly fitted clothing, expensive fabrics, polished boots, carefully styled hair, and a subtle family pin or gold chain. Charming rather than foppish, he is an ambitious heir to an old merchant family.

Councilors disagree. Evidence, reputation, public support, and economic consequences can change votes. The Council is not a single NPC with one predetermined answer.

#### The Temple

Bellweather's temporary civic temple serves multiple faiths until larger sanctuaries are reclaimed. **Bishop Brann** and several **Acolytes** provide ordinary healing, funeral rites, and spiritual support. Significant magic requires a donation, a favor, or access to a higher-tier priest rather than a fixed service price list.

After Blackstone Temple is reclaimed, several congregations debate stewardship of the larger site. That discussion is an early example of Bellweather having to decide whether reclamation means restoration of old ownership, new public use, or shared control.

### How to Use This Chapter

This is the recurring campaign hub. Use it between expeditions rather than as a single linear adventure. After every major chapter, show one visible improvement in Bellweather and one new tension created by recovery.

### Ledger House Between Expeditions

Ledger House is the campaign’s emotional anchor and a reliable source of street-level information.

#### What Happens

Use ordinary patrons to tell the party what changed because of their last mission.

Let rumors from the chapter’s existing tables surface naturally through workers, merchants, guards, refugees, and travelers.

Give the party room to rest, plan, and disagree without an urgent clock unless the next chapter specifically creates one.

#### NPC Behavior

Brakkus offers information and shelter, not orders. He is most useful when the characters ask who is affected, who is hiring, or what happened while they were gone.

#### Clues and Discoveries

Brakkus hears practical rumors but should not know hidden villain plans.

#### Consequences

Relationships made here can provide later testimony, shelter, supplies, or political support.

### The Council Hall

The Council handles public contracts, petitions, payments, property claims, and security.

#### What Happens

Wern Ursen focuses on defense and civilian safety. Bishop Brann focuses on refugees, burial rights, and religious sites. Perrin Cardon argues that restored property and commerce are necessary for Bellweather to survive.

When the party brings evidence, the Council can authorize guards, funds, legal access, or investigations. Bureaucracy takes time and creates records.

#### NPC Behavior

Perrin begins as competent and useful, not sinister. Wern, a dwarf, respects direct plans. Bishop Brann asks who bears the cost of a decision.

#### Clues and Discoveries

Official decisions leave paper trails that become important during Perrin’s later betrayal.

#### Consequences

Council support can make an expedition easier, but seeking it is always optional.

### Markets and Downtime

Bellweather should feel more alive after each successful reclamation.

#### What Happens

Add reopened stalls, workshops, inns, shrines, and construction crews as routes become safe.

Allow training, research, shopping, carousing, crafting, or faction work between missions.

#### Consequences

Downtime spent helping a faction can improve its attitude or create a useful contact.

### City Change

At the end of each expedition, choose one improvement and one tension tied directly to what was reclaimed.

#### What Happens

Improvements include reopened streets, repaired wells, restored shrines, safer river traffic, new housing, or new markets.

Tensions include rent increases, competing deeds, displaced squatters, private guards, tax disputes, labor shortages, or arguments about who paid the risk of reclamation.

#### Consequences

The city never resets. Later chapters should refer back to earlier decisions whenever practical.

These rewards are party totals for the campaign's four-character party.

- **Spendable wealth.** No automatic chapter payout.
- **Consumables.** Purchased normally through available markets.
- **Permanent magic.** None automatically.
- **Story reward.** Downtime, shopping, favors, faction access, and the benefits of a growing Bellweather.

## Running the Chapter

**Primary objectives.** Rescue the survey crew, identify actual threats, establish safe routes, decide how squatters and residents are treated.

**Player agency.** A second route into the district, negotiation with unaffiliated residents, or direct clearing are all valid.

**Fail forward.** The survey crew can always be found; failures mean injury, lost equipment, or hostile attention.

**Rest and pressure.** Safe long rests are available back at Ledger House. Inside the active adventure site, resting gives opposing factions time to reposition unless the characters secure a defensible refuge.

### Opening Route Through the Slums

After the storm and Ledger House, Brakkus points out several ways capable people can find work. Council reclamation is the official route, but a private survey crew, residents, merchants, or simple exploration can all pull the party toward the Slums. Run **2–3 meaningful encounters**, not a block-by-block extermination. **Suggested opening sequence:** first, a mixed scavenger crew searching an abandoned shop, with one scavenger carrying a bundle of brass fittings and door hardware. Treat this primarily as a social and choice encounter; negotiation, intimidation, observation, retreat, or combat are all valid, and the scavengers should not attack simply because the party approaches. Second, route the party through a collapsed residence where a hungry **Grick** provides the reliable combat beat for the session. This combat should occur before the party reaches the survey crew even if the scavengers are resolved peacefully. If time remains, reveal the trail toward the missing survey crew and end on that clue. Save the giant-spider warehouse encounter and the survey-crew rescue for later in the chapter. Negotiation, retreat, and cooperation remain valid outcomes whenever the opposition is intelligent and not committed to violence.

*Map of the Slums.*

The Slums are the first reclamation district and should begin after the storm and Ledger House scenes. The source describes this district as former lower- and middle-class housing now serving as a buffer between the reclaimed quarter and hostile-held parts of the old city. That social history is important: these are homes and workshops that became dangerous after Bellweather fell, not a neighborhood whose residents are inherently lesser.

The Council's assignment is simple: push the safe line outward, identify structures that can be occupied, rescue people in danger, and neutralize immediate threats. The party does not have to kill every creature in the district. Negotiated departures, alliances, surrender, and identifying harmless residents all count toward reclamation.

### The Survey Crew Rescue

Use one early encounter to establish the personal cost of reclamation. A small mixed-species Bellweather survey crew has been sent beyond the safest cleared line to mark property boundaries and structural hazards. A collapse or territorial creature has trapped one worker while another is injured.

The workers are ordinary people. They do not become recurring plot devices unless the players take an interest in them. Their presence simply establishes that the Council's maps, deeds, and recovery plans depend on people doing dangerous work before a district is safe.

Afterward, a worker can ask a practical question such as, **“Do they already know who gets these buildings?”** Keep it to a single practical question; let the question sit beside the visible fact that reclamation creates both safety and valuable property.

#### Optional Jarrock Thread: Rena Dask

Rena Dask, a dwarf stonemason and informal crew representative, can be part of the survey/reclamation workforce without changing the encounter roster or map. She helps stabilize the injured and immediately returns to practical work.

If Jarrock engages her, Rena asks a simple question rather than making a speech: **"When we make one of these buildings safe enough to own again, who gets paid for making it worth owning?"** Let the answer remain unresolved for now.

## Adventure

### Location and Background Details

The Slum District was once a neighborhood of lower- and middle-class dwellings. Since Bellweather established the Reclaimed Quarter, this district has become a dangerous buffer between settled streets and territories controlled by scattered factions, predators, squatters, and desperate scavengers. The Slums are the next section targeted for reclamation, and some residents have already begun repairing homes and shops nearest the gate. No single ancestry or species defines the opposition here. Some inhabitants are hostile, some frightened, some opportunistic, and some simply trying to survive. Use the **Unaligned Ruin District** encounters in Appendix A.

### Scene 1: Entering the District

**Map areas:** A1 — Boundary Crossing|3a1|x and B2 — Residents / Mera Kest Area|3b2|x. Begin at A1; B2 holds the nearby residents and local information that points deeper into the district.

The Slums are inhabited ruins, not an empty dungeon. The first challenge is determining who is dangerous and who is merely trying to live there.

#### What Happens

Use the Player Map for navigation and the DM Keyed Map for encounter locations.

Four scavengers pick through a ruined storefront and the rubble around it; use **Bandit** stat blocks if combat breaks out. One carries a club, one carries a bundle of brass fittings and door hardware, and two keep salvaged items partly hidden behind their backs. They are wary and defensive but do not attack first. They prefer talking, bluffing, bargaining, or backing away to a fight.

Nearby residents watch how the characters respond.

#### NPC Behavior

Mera Kest, a human woman, speaks for the bandits. She is greedy rather than loyal to a cause and bargains if outmatched. Residents answer questions if treated as residents rather than suspects.

#### Encounter

4 scavengers (use **Bandit** stat blocks). They fight only if threatened, attacked, cornered, or convinced the party intends to rob or arrest them. If combat begins, they flee or surrender when two are incapacitated or when an escape route opens.

#### Clues and Discoveries

Mera saw the missing survey crew head toward the old dye works. She has heard clicking in the rafters near a collapsed warehouse.

#### Consequences

Killing surrendering bandits harms local goodwill. Driving them off or bargaining leaves witnesses who may help later.

### Scene 2: Collapsed Residence

**Map areas:** C3 — Collapsed Residence|3c3|x and D4 — Predator / Hazard Zone|3d4|x. Run the grick encounter at C3 as the opening reliable combat beat. D4 is adjoining rubble on the route toward the dye works and foreshadows the deeper danger without adding another mandatory fight.

The route deeper into the district passes through a collapsed residence where a hungry grick has learned to ambush anything moving through the rubble.

#### What Happens

Broken masonry and fallen furniture create patches of difficult terrain. A character who studies the rubble spots signs of a lurking predator without a roll; otherwise use DC 13 Wisdom (Perception) to notice the grick before it attacks.

#### Encounter

1 **Grick**. It is hungry, territorial, and not interested in negotiation. If the party is badly depleted from the storm, it begins in a poor position rather than gaining surprise.

#### Clues and Discoveries

A dead scavenger carries survey ribbon. Drag marks show at least two surveyors left the warehouse alive.

#### Consequences

Destroying the nest makes this route safe for later labor crews.

### Scene 3: The Dye Works

**Map areas:** D4 — Predator / Hazard Zone|3d4|x, E4 — Old Dye Works|3e4|x, and E5 — Missing Survey Crew Site|3e5|x. Beyond this point, webbing and movement around D4 lead toward the dye works, where Lessa Dorn and Kav Orin are barricaded at E5.

Two surveyors are barricaded inside while giant spiders hunt through the adjoining rubble and damaged structures.

#### What Happens

The survivors, Lessa Dorn, a half-elf woman, and Kav Orin, a halfling man, are exhausted and nearly out of water. Their third member, Harl, a human man, left to seek help. Their field satchel contains 2 Potions of Healing carried as emergency medical supplies; the party can recover it regardless of the rescue outcome.

**NPC description — Lessa Dorn and Kav Orin.** Lessa is a lean half-elf woman in early middle age, sun-browned and visibly angry beneath a layer of dust, with a torn survey coat and a measuring cord still looped at her belt. Kav is a younger halfling man, compact and hollow-eyed from exhaustion, who keeps glancing toward exits as if expecting another attack. Both carry the practical gear of people who spend more time in ruins than offices.

The **Giant Spiders** use webbing and ruined walls to cut off the safest approach to the trapped surveyors.

#### NPC Behavior

Lessa is angry about inadequate protection. Kav wants to leave immediately. Both become useful witnesses if treated well.

#### Encounter

2 **Giant Spiders**; use 1 if the party enters this encounter badly depleted or has not had a safe rest.

#### Clues and Discoveries

The survey crew found chalk marks used by bandits and a newer symbol—three vertical cuts crossed by a diagonal line—on crates that did not belong to them.

#### Consequences

The surveyors can guide Bellweather workers through the district once rescued.

### Scene 4: Harl and the Lost Strongbox

**Map placement:** Place Harl and the strongbox in the rubble between D4|3d4|x and E4|3e4|x; no additional map key is required.

Harl lies pinned beneath a fallen beam near Jorn Milar’s storm-lost strongbox.

#### What Happens

Taking ten minutes and using leverage frees Harl automatically. Under pressure, DC 12 Strength (Athletics) frees him quickly.

The strongbox contains 40 gp and mundane trade papers.

#### NPC Behavior

Harl is embarrassed that he left the others but did so to seek help, not abandon them.

#### Clues and Discoveries

Harl saw armed strangers carrying supply crates deeper east. They were not the local bandits.

#### Consequences

Returning the strongbox earns Jorn’s promised payment. Saving Harl earns worker goodwill.

### Scene 5: Return to Bellweather

The party returns with rescued surveyors, a reopened route, and evidence that someone is supplying multiple groups.

#### What Happens

The Council honors any contract. Residents ask whether reopening the district means eviction. Perrin congratulates the party and notes that safe roads make every other recovery possible.

#### NPC Behavior

Perrin should sound reasonable; he is not yet a villain in presentation.

#### Clues and Discoveries

The recurring crossed-line symbol becomes the campaign’s first indication of coordination.

#### Consequences

The characters remain level 3 through Blackstone Temple. If using milestones, advance them to level 4 after Blackstone Temple is resolved. Blackstone Temple becomes the next major problem along the reopened route.

### Transition to Chapter 4

Petitions from residents and clergy report that armed militants have fortified Blackstone Temple and are holding caretakers inside.

In adventure outcome, the transition also centers on the missing survey crew: Blackstone militants knowingly kidnapped the two surviving surveyors. The Council wants them recovered, while Bishop Brann wants the detained temple caretakers rescued and sacred records preserved.

### Rewards

**Treasure and rewards.** The posted reclamation contract pays **75 gp per character** on completion (300 gp for a four-character party). During the expedition, place roughly **200–250 gp** of additional party value in recoverable coin, jewelry, trade goods, legitimate salvage, and private recovery bonuses. The strongbox and similar identifiable property are not free salvage: returning them earns the stated private reward. The survey crew's field satchel at E5 contains 2 Potions of Healing as emergency medical supplies, and the satchel remains recoverable even if the rescue goes badly. Award one useful uncommon consumable such as Dust of Disappearance only if the party explores a sealed workshop; otherwise use mundane salvage.

## Running the Chapter

**First conversation.** Let Nela and the other captives explain the ledger, the altar maintenance passage, and the orders to remove Bishop Brann’s sacred material. Lessa and Kav can identify themselves as the two surviving members of Kaleb’s survey crew and do not yet know Kaleb is dead.

## Adventure

### Scene 1: The Seven Captives

**Nela Orr.** Older human woman; soot-stained gray temple robes; cropped silver hair; firm, controlled voice. Before being recaptured, Nela briefly escaped, stole Tal Rusk’s coded Iron Covenant ledger, locked and hid it in the safe, and returned to captivity under guard.

**What Nela knows.** She knows the maintenance passage behind the altar, overheard orders to pack and remove Bishop Brann’s sacred records and relics, and knows Tal badly wants the coded ledger returned.

**Dwarf caretaker.** Middle-aged, shaved head, ink-stained fingers; exhausted but focused on preserving the temple records.

**Halfling caretaker.** Young, bruised face, torn vestments; frightened but mobile.

**Half-orc caretaker.** Broad-shouldered, one arm in a sling; protective of the other captives.

**Temple guard.** Human, battered mail, bandaged thigh, pale from blood loss but alert. The guard can describe interior movement and confirm Tal still has four Guards.

**Lessa Dorn.** Lean, weathered half-elf Council surveyor. She knows the Iron Covenant abducted the survey crew but does not know Kaleb is dead.

**Kav Orin.** Halfling Council surveyor. He knows the Iron Covenant abducted the survey crew but does not know Kaleb is dead.

**Kaleb.** There is no satchel-recognition trigger. Lessa and Kav can naturally ask whether the party found Kaleb once introductions establish that the characters came through the Slums.

**Nela’s motive.** She will not willingly abandon another captive and prioritizes getting everyone safe while keeping the temple’s sacred material from the Covenant.

**Lessa’s play cue.** She is angry that the survey crew was sent into danger with inadequate protection; she still does not know Kaleb is dead.

**Kav’s play cue.** He wants out of Blackstone alive.

### Scene 2: Tal Rusk and the Ledger

**Tal Rusk.** A compact, scarred dwarf in practical heavy armor, controlled rather than theatrical. He commands four Guards deeper inside Blackstone and is the remaining Iron Covenant commander on site.

**His objective.** Tal’s highest priority is recovering the coded Iron Covenant ledger Pepper holds. He cares more about protecting Covenant intelligence, contacts, and supply information than about dying for the temple.

**Tal’s play cue.** Controlled, professional, and not suicidal. He intimidates first, but his real priority is recovering the ledger and preserving Covenant intelligence; he negotiates when that serves those goals better than combat.

**The bargain.** In exchange for the ledger, Tal can order himself and his four remaining Guards to withdraw from Blackstone. The seven captives remain free, and Bishop Brann’s sacred records and relics remain intact. The escaped gate captain and Guard are already gone and are not part of the bargain.

**If the party keeps the ledger.** Tal will not voluntarily abandon the temple while the ledger remains in the party’s hands. If negotiation fails, run **Tal Rusk** and 4 **Guards** as the decisive Blackstone combat. Tal can surrender or order a retreat if defeated; his force is not suicidal.

**If the ledger is returned.** Tal withdraws with his four Guards. The party gives up the coded intelligence unless it was copied or decoded first, but they keep the rescued captives and recover Bishop Brann’s sacred material.

### Scene 3: Return to Ledger House and the Council

After Blackstone is resolved, Lessa Dorn and Kav Orin report what happened. A Council envoy requires a meeting at Ledger House. A character deliberately listening to the envoy’s private exchange with Brakkus can make a DC 20 Perception check to overhear it.

The Council participants are Ulric Evern, Wern Ursen, Bishop Brann, and Perrin Cardon. Reclamation crews have now searched the Slums and found Kaleb’s body with his head smashed.

#### Council NPC Play Cues

**Brakkus.** Protect the people under his roof; stay calm, dry, practical, and restrained. He does not exist to steer the party’s decisions.

**Ulric Evern.** Budget hawk and hard bargainer; press for accountability and a consequence Bellweather can actually enforce.

**Wern Ursen.** Defense and infrastructure; frame the issue around security, operational risk, and whether the party can still be useful safely.

**Bishop Brann.** Public welfare, healing, burial rights, preservation of sacred records, and humane treatment of prisoners.

**Perrin Cardon.** Polished and ambitious; values useful routes and the political value of keeping effective adventurers working. At this stage he presents as a competent civic figure, not an exposed villain.

**Council envoy.** Procedural messenger only.

Brakkus may tell the envoy that Pepper killed Kaleb if he knows that fact. The Council then questions the party, but there is no scripted confession or predetermined evidentiary outcome; play the scene from what the characters say and what the Council can establish.

**Pepper consequence.** If the Council establishes that Pepper murdered Kaleb, Pepper must complete five mandatory Council missions total: the first is unpaid and the next four are paid normally. Refusal or abandonment of the required service means jail. This sentence applies to Pepper, not automatically to the rest of the party.

**Later emotional beat.** Kaleb’s wife can later confront Pepper at Ledger House while seeking help for her ill daughter. The daughter’s condition can be cured by Greater Restoration. Helping is Pepper’s choice and is not part of the sentence.

### Transition to Chapter 5

All four characters have already agreed to Kellan’s Well. The Council’s reason to act is independent of the ledger: separate armed groups use coordinated signals and routes there, and the catacombs/waterworks are strategically important.

Kellan’s Well is the first of Pepper’s five mandatory Council missions and is unpaid for Pepper. Her remaining four required missions are paid normally. Refusal or abandonment means jail.

## The Iron Covenant at Koven House

Koven House is **an Iron Covenant headquarters**.

The Covenant is not a dragon cult and does not worship Tyren. Its leadership believes it has built a profitable criminal coalition with political protection and unusually reliable financing.

Perrin’s original purpose was political. Through intermediaries, he encouraged the Covenant to bring competing gangs into one structure. Increased organized crime would make the Council look incapable. When the city became frightened enough, Perrin intended to support the crackdown, destroy the network, and step forward as the person who restored order.

Tyren saw a different opportunity: Perrin had gathered a large number of compromised, connected people into one communication system.

Koven House is where Tyren begins treating that system like a nervous system.

## Getting the Job

The Kellan documents and the coded Blackstone ledger point toward the same place after Council clerks compare routing shorthand.

Lessa Dorn and Kav Orin can assist by placing old property boundaries over the recovered delivery routes. Their work establishes that multiple routes terminate at the same mansion compound: Koven House.

Ulric authorizes a warrant once the documentary connection is strong enough.

Wern wants the party to move before the Covenant burns records.

Perrin agrees that immediate action is justified. This is not an act. He genuinely wants this Covenant node broken now that he knows the Spirit is issuing instructions through it without him.

## Table-Ready Opposition

Spread the defenders across the house rather than fighting them all at once.

Use approximately:

- 4 **Spies**;
- 4 **Thugs**;
- 2 **Scouts**;
- 1 **Bandit Captain** as the Covenant Factor.

Adjust counts for party resources. Defenders use hidden routes, cover, doors, and retreat plans. They bargain or flee once the command structure is clearly broken.

The Factor is a local operator rather than a campaign-long villain. The Factor is a local operator.

## 1. Outer Yard

The mansion exterior looks better maintained than surrounding ruins. Someone has money, workers, and reasons to preserve it.

Characters watching for an hour see couriers arrive separately and leave by different routes. The couriers wear no common uniform.

One detail is shared: every courier touches a black iron-nail cross fixed beneath the gate lintel before being admitted.

## 2. Auxiliary Buildings

Use these for surveillance, a hidden service entrance, or a neutral creature/squatter that has learned to avoid the Covenant.

If using the existing quickling encounter seed, keep the quickling **unnamed and nonrecurring**. Respectful characters can learn that people inside sometimes walk through the house at night “like they’re dreaming.”

## 3. Service Corridors

The Iron Covenant uses concealed corridors to move between rooms, attack from advantage, and retreat.

A character who captures a map, spends ten minutes searching a known section, or follows a withdrawing defender can discover a route without needing a single pass/fail roll.

The first important cache contains duplicates of Kellan’s shift schedules and the same color coding.

## 4. Prison and Blackmail Rooms

The Covenant keeps debtors, informants, and blackmail targets here. Most prisoners know only ordinary criminal information.

One prisoner reports something useful: the Covenant Factor recently punished a lieutenant for issuing an order the Factor swore he never gave. Three witnesses heard the Factor’s voice give the instruction.

The Factor blamed impersonation.

This is the first evidence that the impossible coordination may involve more than couriers and paperwork.

## 5. Ledger Room

The ledgers establish the Covenant’s structure.

The essential discoveries are automatic once the room is secured:

- competing gangs were paid bonuses for operating under shared schedules;
- the Covenant financed neutral meeting sites and compensated gangs for settling old feuds;
- money entered the network through ordinary-looking commercial channels;
- one channel leads to a Cardon-connected intermediary, but the paper trail stops before proving Perrin ordered crimes;
- several payments were authorized using a code **not found anywhere in the Covenant’s own key**.

That unknown code appears only on instructions that produced no obvious profit: moving people to particular buildings, changing patrol routes, gathering crowds, or keeping specific streets busy at specific times.

These are Tyren’s experiments.

## 6. Covenant Factor

The Factor uses a **Bandit Captain** stat block and has 2 **Spies** nearby if the party reaches the command room before breaking the house response.

### Portraying the Factor

The Factor looks more like a prosperous fixer than a street criminal: good coat, clean hands, expensive pen, and a small visible piece of Iron Covenant ironwork worn as if it were a business credential. The Factor speaks softly and assumes everyone has a price, a fear, or a practical reason to compromise.

This NPC should feel dangerous because of **competence and information**, not because of theatrical cruelty. The Factor knows names, debts, schedules, who can be leaned on, and which local disputes can be turned into leverage. The unnerving crack in that confidence is the command problem inside the Covenant: orders have been given in familiar voices without the supposed speakers remembering them.

When pressed on those incidents, the Factor becomes defensive in a very specific way—less "that is impossible" and more "someone has compromised my system." The Factor still believes there is a human explanation.

The Factor knows the following:

- an outside patron paid the Covenant to unify criminal operations;
- the patron never appeared personally;
- the arrangement was supposed to be profitable and politically protected;
- somebody has recently begun issuing orders through the network without paying and without using recognized Covenant authority;
- people who receive those orders often insist afterward that they made the choice themselves.

The Factor assumes a rival has compromised the organization.

If captured alive, the Factor can identify the commercial intermediary but cannot identify Perrin.

## 7. Command Cache

A hidden command cache contains the best evidence and one disturbing object: a page where several different hands have copied the same sentence in nearly identical wording.

> **We know where we are needed. We will be there.**

None of the writers remember being told to write it.

Present this as contradictory testimony and impossible command behavior; the player-facing description does not identify it as mind control.

## Treasure

The house contains approximately **1,500 gp** in portable criminal wealth, much of it identifiable as stolen property.

Use one memorable permanent uncommon item, two useful spell scrolls of 3rd level or lower, and two consumables. Returning identifiable property improves the party’s standing with victims and merchants.

## Resolution

Koven House is broken when the Factor is captured, killed, driven out, or loses the ability to coordinate the Covenant cell and the party secures the ledgers.

The Iron Covenant survives as an organization unless the party takes extraordinary steps. What breaks here is its central Bellweather headquarters and the political usefulness of this particular network.

### DM Truth: Perrin’s Second Off-Ramp

Perrin now has documentary confirmation that the Spirit is issuing commands through the organization he created.

He also sees a benefit.

The Covenant becomes easier to destroy because frightened criminals are behaving predictably. Perrin can publicly support the raid and privately tell himself that the Spirit merely helped clean up the mess Perrin intended to clean up eventually.

He does not confess.

His rationalization changes from **“I didn’t authorize that”** to **“the result was still useful.”**

## Why the Party Goes

Perrin publicly asks the Council to authorize an expedition. His reasons are openly mixed:

- the records may clarify the Tyren mystery;
- surviving machinery could create jobs;
- the building belongs to his family under old deeds;
- restoring a major Cardon enterprise would bring capital into Bellweather.

None of those claims is automatically false.

This is exactly why the chapter matters.

## Perrin’s Public Position

Perrin does not hide that he wants his family property back.

He argues that Bellweather needs private investment, functioning businesses, and people willing to risk money on recovery. He promises to reopen the Weavehouse rather than leave it as a private ruin.

If the party challenges him on profiting from Council risk, he does not become sinister. He argues the point.

## 1. Approach

The Weavehouse is occupied by a mixed group of squatters, hired guards, and opportunists using portions of the structure.

Separate people by behavior rather than species.

Some occupants will leave if offered safe passage. Some workers claim they have maintained machinery for years and deserve a share. A small armed faction intends to sell the contents and resists negotiation.

## 2. Loom Hall

The main loom floor is dangerous because of weakened catwalks and partially functioning mechanisms.

Use environmental hazards rather than random monsters:

- broken catwalk sections;
- suspended weights;
- jammed gear trains;
- unstable stored dyes.

A fight here should reward movement and creative use of machinery.

**Encounter option.** 1 **Gladiator**-equivalent leader, 2 **Veterans**, and 2 **Scouts**, distributed between floor and catwalks. They surrender or flee when the leader falls and escape remains possible.

## 3. Worker Quarters

Several current occupants are noncombatants who have been living in the building.

They know Cardon agents have already visited and offered employment if Perrin regains legal control.

Most of those offers are real.

This is one of the first visible examples of Perrin using influence for something that produces a genuinely positive result. People who were suspicious of him become enthusiastic after short private conversations.

PC saves do not occur here. The effect is on NPCs and should still look like charisma, hope, or politics.

## 4. Family Archive

The archive contains Cardon deeds, partnership papers, and proof that the family once held substantial legitimate property.

It also contains emergency ledgers showing that the Weavehouse supplied food, cloth, and shelter during the first days of the old crisis.

Perrin can point to this history as evidence that the Cardons did not merely enrich themselves; they helped hold the city together.

A separate civic ledger references transfers to Marren Library during the last week before the fall.

That is the next major historical lead.

## 5. Hidden Cardon Reserve

A family reserve of portable wealth survived behind a sealed wall.

Use a meaningful but controlled amount: **2,000–3,000 gp equivalent** in coin, trade bars, gems, and negotiable records, plus one family heirloom magic item appropriate to the tier.

Legally, most of it belongs to Perrin under the recognized deed unless the party or Council successfully challenges that claim.

Recovering it materially changes Perrin’s circumstances.

This is the point where the Cardon fortune stops being a nostalgic ambition and becomes real economic power.

## The Ownership Decision

The party does not decide Bellweather law by fiat, but its testimony and actions matter.

Possible outcomes include:

- recognize Perrin’s full claim;
- require compensation or tenancy rights for current workers;
- place historical records in public custody while returning commercial assets to Perrin;
- negotiate a mixed public/private operation;
- challenge the claim and force a Council hearing.

Perrin remembers how the party treats him here.

He does not need to become their enemy if they oppose him.

## Bellweather Changes

Within weeks, if Perrin receives enough of the property, the Weavehouse reopens in some form.

Jobs appear.

Merchants invest.

The Cardon name becomes associated with visible recovery.

Perrin’s wealth and public approval increase together.

This is a success story.

That fact is essential to his corruption.

### DM Truth: Rewarding the Hidden Desire

Tyren’s gift appears vindicated.

Perrin can now look at a functioning business, paid workers, increasing trade, and restored family wealth and conclude that his willingness to do what cautious people would not do is exactly why Bellweather is improving.

The hidden desire for power is no longer merely hidden. Perrin begins enjoying being the person who can make outcomes happen.

## Pepper: Mission 4 Complete

Mark **Mission 4 of 5 complete** when the authorized objective is resolved and Pepper returns as required.

## Milestone

Advance the party to **level 6** after the Savin Keep + Cardon Weavehouse arc is resolved and Perrin’s restored wealth becomes a visible part of Bellweather politics.

# Chapter 9: Marren Library

**Recommended level:** 6  
**Expected milestone:** 7 after the Marren/Mansion phase  
**Likely table time:** 1–2 sessions

## Modern Running Sheet

| | |
|---|---|
| **Chapter purpose** | Reveal what Tyren can do without yet revealing what Tyren physically is. Finish Pepper’s five-mission Council sentence. |
| **Primary objectives** | Preserve the archive, recover the Tyren material, stop a targeted theft/burning of records, decide who controls copies. |
| **Player agency** | Work with scholars, Council, Cardon interests, or independent custodians; split priorities; protect records rather than simply clear rooms. |
| **Fail forward** | Core Tyren and Radiant Cage clues survive in more than one source. |
| **Pepper consequence** | Mission 5 of 5. Paid normally. Successful completion ends the mandatory mission sentence and house arrest. |
| **Story revelation** | Tyren can occupy living hosts; the Radiant Well was used to contain him; killing a host does not necessarily kill Tyren. |

![](img/adventure/NCFA/marren_library.png)

*Use the existing Marren Library map and archive geography.*

## Table-Ready Encounter Package

> The Library is a knowledge-and-control chapter. Books and records are the primary treasure and the possession reveal is the primary payoff.
>
> **Guardians:** use 1–2 encounters from **Animated Armor**, **Flying Sword**, **Specter**, **Shadow**, **Mimic**, **Nothic**, or **Gibbering Mouther**.
>
> Avoid stacking every listed creature into one expedition. Choose threats that fit the rooms the players actually explore.
>
> **Fire / collapse pressure** should create choices about what records can be saved rather than simply dealing damage.

### Rewards and Treasure

Award 2–4 level-appropriate spell scrolls, one uncommon or rare utility item, and valuable manuscripts worth about **500 gp** if sold. Their political and historical value should usually exceed the cash price.


### Stat Block Setup

| Role | 5etools lookup / setup |
|---|---|
| Construct guardian | **Animated Armor** / **Flying Sword** |
| Haunting | **Specter** / **Shadow** |
| Opportunistic predator | **Mimic** |
| Lore-bearing strange resident | **Nothic** |
| High-pressure aberrant threat | **Gibbering Mouther** |


## Why the Party Goes

The Savin records and Cardon Weavehouse ledgers independently point to Marren Library as the institution that received sealed magical research during the final week before Old Bellweather fell.

The Council needs the archive before somebody else removes the same material.

Pepper’s fifth mandatory job is therefore not a punishment invented around her. It is a legitimate Council mission that happens to complete her sentence.

## Competing Interests

Three interests arrive at once:

**Council clerks** want dangerous historical records kept in public custody.

**Scholars** want damaged collections stabilized and copied before politics decides ownership.

**Perrin** wants Cardon family papers returned to him and argues that private records should not become permanent Council property simply because they survived in a public archive.

Perrin’s legal position is not automatically unreasonable.

## 1. Garden Court

The overgrown court contains signs of recent passage.

Use 2 **Gargoyles** or another existing guardian encounter only if the party disturbs the central monument. The more important clue is a dropped satchel containing copied shelf numbers from the magical-research wing.

Someone arrived first looking for the same material.

## 2. Damaged Stacks

A fire has begun in one section while hired scavengers remove selected volumes.

This scene should force priorities:

- fight the fire;
- stop the scavengers;
- protect trapped staff or civilians;
- identify what the thieves were sent to steal.

Three successful practical actions contain the fire. Water, blankets, magic, tools, and appropriate ability checks all count.

The scavengers were paid through an anonymous Covenant-style drop. They were told to retrieve books about:

- the Radiant Well;
- “personality transference”;
- civic possession events;
- the word **Tyren**.

They do not know why.

## 3. Master Librarian’s Chamber

A damaged ward protects a locked desk.

Use a DC 15 Intelligence (Arcana) check or *Dispel Magic* to suppress the ward. Failure triggers a short fear effect or environmental pulse; the clue itself is still recovered.

Inside are several damaged records that finally make the supernatural problem explicit.

### Essential Record: Tyren

The clearest surviving journal says, in careful academic language, that **Tyren is an intelligence capable of occupying living hosts**.

It does not identify Tyren’s species.

The author believed:

- Tyren’s influence could spread through repeated psychic contact;
- some people were unusually resistant;
- destroying a controlled host would not necessarily destroy Tyren;
- the Radiant Well was used in the final containment;
- the containment was called the **Radiant Cage** in technical notes.

A margin note reads:

> **The Cage is not a tomb. It is a return. They forced him back into himself.**

## 4. The Unturned File

A separate file contains testimonies about resistant people.

The testimonies disagree about why resistance occurred. Some resistant people were devout. Some were traumatized veterans. Some were children. Some were ordinary people with no obvious shared trait.

The archivist’s conclusion is deliberately uncertain:

> **“The common quality may not be strength of mind. It may be certainty of self.”**

This is the historical foundation for the anchored-mind rule.

The file never identifies which PCs qualify or why.

## 5. Property Archives

Perrin’s family materials are here as well.

They confirm that the Cardons were among Bellweather’s founders and that their wealth was heavily damaged during the original fall.

They also show that emergency powers transferred property, contracts, and civic obligations in ways that were never fully resolved.

Perrin is especially attentive to these records.

The party may give him originals, copies, or require public duplication first.

## 6. The Missing Volume

One volume is gone before the party arrives.

Its catalog entry describes it only as:

> **VAREN CONTAINMENT — PHYSICAL FORM / LOWER CHAMBER**

This preserves the dragon reveal.

The missing volume has been taken by Tyren-controlled agents through the criminal network because it contains too much information about the body beneath Varen Keep.

The party cannot recover it here.

## Perrin’s Reaction

When the party discusses Tyren and possession, Perrin’s reaction should be controlled but imperfect.

He is disturbed.

He has been calling this presence the Spirit of the City for years. The records tell him that an entity with the same kind of influence was trapped beneath Varen Keep after nearly destroying Bellweather.

He asks whether old frightened officials could have misunderstood a protective spirit.

This is not a calculated lie. He wants that answer to be true.

### DM Truth: Perrin Chooses the Comfortable Explanation

Tyren tells Perrin that the old ruling class feared a force capable of making Bellweather act together and imprisoned the city’s true spirit to preserve their own authority.

Perrin now has enough evidence to walk away.

He does not.

By this point the Spirit’s power has restored his family’s fortune, expanded employment, and made Perrin one of Bellweather’s most effective public figures.

Perrin decides the old records are incomplete and biased.

This is a choice.

## Custody Decision

The party decides who receives the most dangerous records and copies.

Possible custodians include:

- the Council archive;
- Marren scholars;
- Bishop Brann’s religious institution;
- Perrin for Cardon-specific records only;
- multiple copies split among institutions.

Brakkus, if consulted later, strongly favors **redundancy**. His concern is practical: no single person should be able to make dangerous knowledge disappear.

## Pepper: Mission 5 Complete

Successful completion of this assignment marks **Mission 5 of 5**.

Pepper has fulfilled the Council’s sentence.

Her mandatory mission obligation and Ledger House arrest end. She is no longer required to continue adventuring for the Council.

Lessa Dorn and Kav Orin can be present when the final field documentation is accepted. Their role pays off here: they were among the people who reported the original accusation, and they have now also seen Pepper complete the work imposed on her.

Reconciliation is not assumed. Their attitude depends on what Pepper actually did during the five missions.

Pepper’s later encounter with Caleb’s family is **not part of the sentence** and is not another punishment.

## Milestone

The Marren Library revelation is part of the level-6 arc. Advance to **level 7** once the Library and associated Mansion/Cardon property consequences have been meaningfully resolved.

# Interlude: Caleb’s Family

Run this after Marren Library and before or during the next Bellweather-focused arc, when there is enough table space for the scene to matter.

## Kerrik Brings the News

Use the full NPC profiles in **Core Recurring Cast**. This scene works best if Kerrik, Mara, and Elise feel like people with conflicting needs rather than instruments for Pepper's character development.

**Kerrik**, an aarakocra and Caleb’s closest friend, comes to Ledger House looking specifically for Pepper.

He is not there to arrest Pepper, threaten her, or extend the sentence.

He tells her that Caleb’s daughter, **Elise**, is seriously ill and ordinary healing has not fixed it.

Kerrik does not like asking Pepper for anything. He is doing it because Caleb’s widow has run out of options and Pepper is a cleric with access to stronger magic.

## Mara and Elise

**Mara** is human, angry, exhausted, and direct.

She does not forgive Pepper because Pepper arrived with healing magic.

Her request is emotionally simple:

> **“You killed my husband. I know what the Council says happened. I also know you are a cleric, and my daughter is sick. Help her.”**

Mara’s request remains separate from Council terms, reputation, payment, alignment, and future quests.

Pepper may help or refuse.

That choice belongs to the player.

## Elise’s Affliction

Elise is not possessed and Tyren did not deliberately target her.

Her illness is **psychic spillover** from the expanding network Tyren has built through influenced people in Bellweather.

Symptoms can include:

- severe headaches;
- insomnia;
- brief fugue states;
- answering questions nobody asked aloud;
- beginning a simple action and then stopping, confused about why she started;
- repeating a phrase heard elsewhere in the network.

Ordinary healing restores hit points but does not remove the condition.

Treat the affliction as a supernatural mental curse/possession residue removable by **Greater Restoration** or equivalent story-appropriate high magic.

If Pepper uses *Greater Restoration*, Elise recovers.

The spell also reveals something useful: for a moment, Pepper senses that the affliction was not rooted inside Elise. It was pressure **passing through** her from somewhere else.

That makes the family scene a personal clue without making Caleb’s murder part of Tyren’s plan.

# Chapter 10: The Mansion District

**Recommended level:** 6–7  
**Milestone:** Shared with Marren Library; none for clearing houses  
**Likely table time:** optional / merged material

## Purpose

The Mansion District survives as a major named part of Bellweather, but it is no longer a mandatory reclamation chapter.

Use it for private commissions, inheritance conflicts, hidden vaults, social consequences of Perrin’s rise, and side adventures when the players choose to engage with wealthy families or old records.

The district is valuable because it shows Bellweather becoming prosperous **without every problem being Tyren**.

## Three Adventure Models

### Legitimate Inheritance

A family hires the party to recover heirlooms from a dangerous house. The claim is genuine. The danger inside is also genuine.

The interesting question is whether current occupants, workers, or tenants have developed legitimate interests in the property during the decades the old family was absent.

### Fraudulent Claim

A wealthy claimant produces convincing paperwork for a mansion and vault that do not actually belong to them.

The adventure is investigation plus danger, not merely combat.

### Public Promise

A recovered deed reveals that a wealthy family once promised part of an estate for a hospice, school, shrine, or public garden. Modern heirs want the valuable property but not the obligation.

The party’s testimony matters.

## Perrin in the District

Perrin becomes increasingly influential among returning families and investors.

He does not have to control everyone he persuades.

Some people support him because:

- he restored a major business;
- he has money;
- he gets things done;
- he is charismatic;
- his policies benefit them.

Use supernatural influence sparingly here. The point is that Perrin no longer needs magic for every victory.

## Story Use

The Mansion District can provide:

- Cardon family history;
- old civic correspondence;
- wealthy political allies;
- evidence of how much Perrin’s social standing has changed;
- unrelated treasure hunts and monster encounters;
- a place where players can choose to oppose, support, or negotiate with returning wealth.

Clearing houses by itself does not award a milestone.

# Chapter 11: Paden Square

**Recommended level:** 7  
**Milestone:** None by itself  
**Likely table time:** 1–2 sessions

## Modern Running Sheet

| | |
|---|---|
| **Chapter purpose** | Show Tyren controlling a large group at once and introduce the first Command-level pressure on the PCs without pointing directly at Perrin. |
| **Primary objectives** | Protect civilians, identify why supposedly separate groups move together, stop a coordinated seizure of the square, preserve evidence. |
| **Player agency** | Infiltration, staged trade, crowd management, rooftop approach, negotiation, sabotage, or direct combat. |
| **Fail forward** | The mass-control event happens visibly enough that the party can investigate it even if leaders escape. |
| **Story revelation** | Tyren can move a crowd like one organism. Anchored minds resist differently. |

![](img/adventure/NCFA/paden_square.png)

*Use the existing Paden Square map and marketplace layout.*

## Table-Ready Encounter Package

> Paden Square is the first large public demonstration of Tyren controlling many people at once. The encounter should feel chaotic and uncanny rather than like a standard army fight.
>
> **Controlled core:** 1 **Veteran** or **Bandit Captain**, 4 **Thugs/Guards**, and 2 **Scouts/Spies**.
>
> **Crowd pressure:** treat civilians as moving hazards, rescue objectives, blocked routes, and frightened witnesses—not as hit-point bags.
>
> **PC/Jord control:** at chosen dramatic moments, call for a Wisdom saving throw. Failure produces only one *Command*-style compelled action. Control immediately ends after that action.

### Rewards and Treasure

The reward is primarily evidence, reputation, and access. Use 500–1,000 gp in recovered payments/contraband plus 1–2 consumables if the group needs a material reward.


### Stat Block Setup

| Role | 5etools lookup / setup |
|---|---|
| Controlled organizer | **Veteran** or **Bandit Captain** |
| Controlled muscle | **Thug** / **Guard** |
| Rooftop observer | **Scout** |
| Courier / infiltrator | **Spy** |
| PCs and Jord | Use Appendix: Anchored Mind overlay only |


## The Setup

Paden Square is functioning as a busy commercial center again.

Perrin sees an opportunity to accelerate his political strategy. Through surviving criminal intermediaries, he encourages several crews to stage thefts, intimidation, and a public failure of security during a crowded market day.

His intended sequence is cynical but limited:

1. visible disorder embarrasses the Council;
2. Wern responds too aggressively;
3. Perrin argues for competent unified leadership;
4. Perrin supports a crackdown and earns public trust.

Tyren hijacks the event.

## 1. Market Tension

Begin with ordinary problems: pickpockets, nervous guards, rival merchants, rumors that the Council cannot keep the square safe.

The scene begins as ordinary public tension; supernatural behavior appears later.

The party may learn that several groups known to dislike each other are present at the same time.

## 2. The Trigger

At a chosen moment, the square changes.

Several criminals stop whatever they are doing and look in the same direction.

Then they move.

No shouted order passes between them.

Some civilians also begin performing simple coordinated actions: closing shutters, blocking exits, moving carts, or walking toward preselected positions.

The movements are not tactically perfect. They are unnaturally unified.

## 3. First Anchored-Mind Saves

This is the recommended first use of unexplained Tyren saves on the PCs.

Ask each PC for a **Wisdom saving throw** at a moment when the pressure reaches the whole square.

Describe only the Wisdom save unless the characters have already established that language through play.

On a failure, use one brief Command-like imperative appropriate to the scene:

- **Halt** — the character spends the turn unable to advance;
- **Drop** — the character drops one held nonessential object;
- **Flee** — the character uses movement to get away from the center of the square;
- **Approach** — the character moves toward a marked point or doorway;
- **Yield** — the character steps aside and allows someone to pass.

The compelled action never forces an attack, spell expenditure, consumable use, or irreversible action.

The player has full control immediately afterward.

Jord, if present, can also make the save and may fail in a mildly comic but useful way: he starts following an imperative, catches himself, and angrily asks who agreed to these terms.

## 4. The Unturned Pattern Reappears

Bishop Brann, if present in the square or brought into the aftermath, is unaffected.

Brakkus is also unaffected if circumstances place him nearby.

Let the immunity emerge through contrast rather than announcement. Let the contrast exist.

A character who compares the event to Marren’s Unturned records has enough information to form the hypothesis that some people are harder for Tyren to seize.

## 5. Combat and Objectives

Use a mixed force appropriate to level 9–10 rather than a mob of dozens.

Suggested active combatants:

- 1 **Bandit Captain** or **Veteran** field leader;
- 2 **Veterans**;
- 4 **Scouts/Spies**;
- controlled civilians as **terrain complications**, not enemy hit-point bags.

Objectives may include:

- open an exit;
- prevent a wagon blockade;
- stop a controlled courier;
- protect trapped civilians;
- capture a leader alive;
- reach a command document before it burns.

## 6. The Evidence

The criminals carried ordinary Covenant instructions for disorder.

Those instructions do **not** explain the mass synchronization.

Surviving criminals are as frightened as anyone else. Several insist they remember deciding to move but cannot explain why.

One says:

> **“It felt like I’d already decided before I knew there was a choice.”**

That sentence should matter later.

## Perrin’s Reaction

Perrin publicly condemns the disorder and supports relief for merchants and civilians.

He privately understands exactly what he saw: the Spirit took the network Perrin built and moved it as one body.

This is the clearest warning he has received.

### DM Truth: Perrin’s Greatest Off-Ramp

Perrin now knows that the Spirit can override groups directly.

He knows the old records described exactly this kind of event.

He knows people were hurt.

He could expose the bargain, surrender his political future, and ask Brann or the party for help.

He chooses not to.

The reason is no longer ignorance.

Part of him is horrified.

Part of him is impressed.

## Wern’s Response

Wern advocates emergency measures: controlled entry points, more armed patrols, temporary curfews around vulnerable markets, and a unified chain of command.

Some of these measures are defensible. Some are excessive.

Perrin does not need to fabricate Wern’s severity. He simply lets people see it and positions himself as the calm alternative.

## No Cause for Alarm Beat

Perrin addresses merchants and residents after the crisis.

He acknowledges the attack, praises the response, and insists Bellweather remains strong.

He ends with a familiar civic reassurance:

> **“We will not hand this city back to fear. Bellweather is open, Bellweather is growing, and there is no cause for alarm.”**

This is the first time the phrase should feel deliberately political.

## Milestone

Paden Square does not grant a level by itself. Keep the party at **level 7**; Paden Square and Storen Gate together form the next escalation milestone.

# Chapter 12: Storen Gate

**Recommended level:** 7  
**Expected milestone:** 8  
**Likely table time:** 1–2 sessions

## Modern Running Sheet

| | |
|---|---|
| **Chapter purpose** | Give Perrin the first situation in which he knowingly asks Tyren to directly control someone because persuasion feels too slow. |
| **Primary objectives** | Resolve a genuine security crisis at the gate, prevent outside forces from exploiting Bellweather’s instability, identify compromised orders. |
| **Player agency** | Defend, infiltrate, negotiate, sabotage signals, evacuate civilians, or expose false orders. |
| **Fail forward** | Failure changes control of sections of the gate and creates casualties; it does not end the campaign. |
| **Story revelation** | Direct control can solve a real problem, which becomes Perrin’s justification for using it voluntarily. |

![](img/adventure/NCFA/storen_gate.png)

*Use the existing Storen Gate map and strategic gate terrain.*

## Table-Ready Encounter Package

> Storen Gate should force Perrin to choose direct control because persuasion is too slow. The security crisis must be real enough that his choice can initially look effective.
>
> **Gate command:** 1 **Knight** or **Veteran**, 2 **Veterans**, and 4 **Guards**, distributed across the gate complex rather than fought at once.
>
> **Optional attacking pressure:** use 4–6 level-appropriate attackers from a named hostile force if the scene needs a true two-sided crisis.
>
> The party can win through control of the gate mechanism, negotiation, sabotage, rescue, or defeating the command element.

### Rewards and Treasure

Use the Council's civic reward plus one practical permanent or consumable item appropriate to level 10–11. The main reward is authority, evidence, and changed Council relationships.


### Stat Block Setup

| Role | 5etools lookup / setup |
|---|---|
| Gate commander | **Knight** or **Veteran** |
| Veteran squad | **Veteran** |
| Rank-and-file | **Guard** |
| Signal / wall lookout | **Scout** |


## The Crisis

Storen Gate is not attacked because “the next district needs reclaiming.”

The crisis develops because Bellweather’s recent instability has become visible beyond the city. A hostile force, smuggling coalition, or armed regional faction tests the gate at the same time Wern has concentrated guards elsewhere after Paden Square.

The threat is real.

If the party does nothing, people may die and Bellweather may lose a critical route.

## 1. Conflicting Orders

When the party arrives, the gate garrison has received contradictory instructions.

One order says hold the gate closed.

Another says admit a relief convoy.

Both appear to carry legitimate Council authorization.

Ulric’s office insists only one was issued.

Wern’s officers accuse civilian clerks of interference.

The confusion echoes Savin’s historical records without duplicating them exactly.

## 2. The Relief Convoy

The approaching convoy contains a mix of legitimate supplies and hostile infiltrators.

The party can discover the problem through inspection, scouts, magic, captured messengers, or simply watching who responds strangely to signals.

Discovery does not hinge on one roll; checks affect speed, detail, or cost.

## 3. Perrin’s Choice

Perrin is at or near the gate because the crisis has become political as well as military.

At a critical point, an officer refuses a risky order Perrin believes is necessary to prevent the infiltrators from seizing the mechanism.

Perrin has alternatives:

- persuade the officer;
- defer to Wern;
- let the party handle it;
- accept the delay.

Instead, in private thought, Perrin asks the Spirit for help.

Tyren takes the officer.

The officer immediately obeys.

The decision works.

Lives are saved because the gate moves at exactly the right moment.

This is the first time Perrin **knowingly requests direct control** rather than merely accepting influence or covering up Tyren’s independent action.

## 4. The Party’s View

The moment is subtle enough to support more than one immediate interpretation.

The officer may simply change their mind abruptly.

Characters watching closely can notice:

- a momentary blankness;
- the officer using wording unlike their normal speech;
- immediate obedience after refusing seconds earlier;
- no visible conversation with Perrin.

An Insight check can establish that the change was unnatural, but it should not identify the source.

If an anchored PC is caught in the same psychic pulse, call for one Wisdom save and use the same one-turn Command ceiling.

## 5. Gate Fight

Run the final gate fight around objectives rather than a long attrition battle.

Use a level-appropriate command group such as:

- 1 **Knight** or **Gladiator**;
- 2 **Veterans**;
- 4 **Scouts/Guards**;
- one specialist such as a **Mage** only if the party is well rested and optimized.

Objectives:

- control the portcullis;
- stop a signal;
- prevent infiltration;
- protect trapped workers;
- hold long enough for reinforcement.

## 6. Aftermath

The city celebrates a real victory.

Perrin can point to the gate and know, truthfully, that his decision saved people.

That is the danger.

### DM Truth: The New Rationalization

Perrin’s internal argument becomes:

> **If I had refused to use the power because of abstract moral fear, people would have died.**

He has now crossed from covering up Tyren’s behavior to **choosing direct control as a tool**.

The next time will be easier.

## Council Fracture

Wern demands broader emergency authority after the gate crisis.

Ulric insists emergency power must remain documented, temporary, and reviewable.

Brann warns that fear does not make consent irrelevant.

Perrin positions himself between them, publicly rejecting both paralysis and overreaction.

His popularity increases.

## Milestone

Advance the party to **level 8** after the Paden Square + Storen Gate escalation is resolved and Perrin has knowingly chosen direct control because it works.

## Table-Ready Encounter Package

> Valen Cemetery is deliberately **not** a Tyren plot. It is a genuine undead crisis that gives Bishop Brann room to matter and demonstrates his immunity without shrinking the world.
>
> Choose 2–3 set pieces using **Ghoul**, **Wight**, **Specter**, **Wraith**, **Zombie**, or another level-appropriate undead mix.

>

### Rewards and Treasure

Keep grave goods restrained. Award one meaningful religious or memorial item, 2 consumables, and civic/temple favor rather than a large tomb hoard.


### Stat Block Setup

| Role | 5etools lookup / setup |
|---|---|
| Fast undead pressure | **Ghoul** |
| Intelligent dead | **Wight** |
| Haunting | **Specter** |
| Chapter controller | **Wraith** |
| Mass background dead | **Zombie** |
| Bishop Brann | **Priest** + Anchored Immunity overlay |


## Independent Threat

The undead crisis at Valen Cemetery is **not caused by Tyren**.

Keep that boundary.

A damaged necromantic focus, restless dead, grave robbery, or an old funerary working has begun producing undead and endangering people who use the cemetery.

The campaign needs problems that matter even when Perrin and Tyren are not responsible.

## Bishop Brann’s Role

Brann asks the party for help because this is his area of responsibility and because ordinary mourners are being endangered.

He should be useful without becoming the adventuring party’s combat solution.

He can:

- provide burial records;
- identify old rites;
- protect civilians;
- consecrate a safe staging area;
- explain what kind of undead the party is facing;
- perform a ritual while the PCs handle the dangerous part.

## 1. Memorial Walk

Begin among ordinary mourners, workers, and families. The cemetery should feel like a place people still need, not an abandoned dungeon.

An undead attack interrupts a burial or reinterment.

Use **Ghouls**, **Wights**, **Specters**, or another level-appropriate undead mix. Keep civilians as rescue objectives rather than combatants.

## 2. Broken Mausoleum

The disturbance traces to old underground burial chambers.

Clues point toward a cracked ritual focus and grave robbers who disturbed material they did not understand.

The robbers may still be alive and terrified. They are criminals, not Tyren agents.

## 3. The Control Pulse

During the investigation, Tyren takes advantage of the party being together and attempts a brief psychic pressure from afar.

This is not the cause of the undead. It is Tyren probing the people who survived Paden.

Call for a Wisdom save from the PCs only if the moment will add tension rather than interrupt a delicate roleplay scene.

On a failure, use the established one-turn Command ceiling.

Brann is unaffected.

If a character watches Brann during the pulse, there is no blankness, hesitation, or struggle. He notices the pressure but it does not move him.

## 4. Brann’s Anchor

Brann does not volunteer his childhood trauma unless trust and context lead him there.

If the party asks why he seems different from them, or if a quiet scene naturally opens the subject, he explains that his faith did not begin as certainty.

When Brann was a child, he watched his sister murdered by a gang.

He spent years deciding what that event would be allowed to make him.

He chose a life built around healing, burial, protection, and service because he refused to let the people who killed her dictate the shape of his soul.

That choice, repeated for decades, is part of why Tyren has so little to grab.

Brann does not describe himself as magically immune. He simply knows the pressure feels like something trying to tell him who he is, and that question was settled a long time ago.

## 5. Necromantic Focus

The final chamber contains the independent source of the undead crisis.

Use one major undead controller and environmental hazards rather than waves of low-level undead.

Possible encounter:

- 1 **Wight** or stronger tier-appropriate undead controller;
- 2–4 **Specters/Ghouls**;
- one ritual hazard that can be disabled with Religion, Arcana, radiant damage, or physical destruction.

The party can win by defeating the controller, destroying the focus, completing the proper rite, or combining those approaches.

## Consequences

Bellweather holds public funerals and reburials after the crisis.

Brann’s standing rises because he helped ordinary people through a genuine civic emergency without demanding more authority for himself.

That contrast matters when Perrin later argues that power should concentrate in the hands of whoever can use it effectively.

## Milestone

Valen Cemetery does not grant a level by itself. Keep the party at **level 8**; it can be played before Maren Isle or held for later.

## Table-Ready Encounter Package

## Independent Crisis, Connected Research

The Storen River is being poisoned by arcane experiments on Maren Isle.

The experimenter is **not working for Tyren**.

The arcanist acquired stolen historical material because research into psychic projection and the Radiant Well was useful to unrelated experiments. The contamination is the consequence of reckless magical engineering, not another Tyren plot.

### Portraying the Arcanist

Keep the arcanist memorable without making them another campaign principal. They are brilliant, sleep-deprived, defensive about the work, and genuinely offended by the suggestion that "reckless" and "unsuccessful" mean the same thing. Their working robes were once expensive but now show singed hems, ink stains, hurried repairs, and pockets full of tools. Protective lenses are usually pushed up onto the forehead until something begins sparking.

The arcanist talks quickly when discussing mechanisms and much more slowly when forced to discuss consequences. They have spent so long treating danger as a solvable technical problem that they initially describe poisoned water and psychic intrusion with the same detached vocabulary.

They are **not working for Tyren**. Their worst decisions came from curiosity, ego, and the belief that understanding a dangerous thing is the same as controlling it.

When the party proves the experiment is hurting people, the arcanist may respond with denial, repair, surrender, bargaining, or escalation. Give them room to choose denial, repair, surrender, bargaining, or doubling down according to how the characters approach them.

This separation is important.

## 1. Following the River

Residents, healers, boat crews, and travelers agree on the basic facts:

- contamination begins near the island;
- ordinary purification is unreliable;
- strange lights appear over the pyramid;
- altered aquatic predators gather near discharge points.

Use **Water Weird**, **Giant Crocodile**, elementals, or other level-appropriate aquatic threats only where they make sense.

## 2. Island Defenses

The island uses magical guardians and engineered defenses rather than an army.

Appropriate opponents include:

- **Gargoyles**;
- **Helmed Horrors**;
- **Invisible Stalker**;
- elementals;
- a **Mage** or other official spellcaster stat block for the arcanist if combat occurs.

The party can negotiate if they reach the arcanist before destroying the laboratory.

## 3. The Pollution System

The contamination comes from a damaged or overdriven magical pumping system.

Run this as an objective problem:

- shut down three feed valves;
- redirect a magical conduit;
- stabilize a cracked reservoir;
- prevent defenders from reactivating the system.

Success cleans the river over the following days.

## 4. Psychic Research Room

The arcanist has been experimenting with defenses against telepathic intrusion because the stolen Varen text described unwanted psychic contact.

This room gives the party concrete rules knowledge.

### Ring of Mind Shielding

One **Ring of Mind Shielding** is present as a research tool or secured item.

For this campaign, a creature wearing the ring is **immune to Tyren’s influence and control** because Tyren must establish unwanted telepathic contact before any of his supernatural control effects can function.

This is a Tyren-specific interaction. All other rules for the item remain unchanged.

### Other Defenses

Research establishes several ways to help against Tyren:

- effects that prevent or end the **Charmed** condition interfere with his ordinary influence;
- *Protection from Evil and Good* does not automatically work simply because Tyren is a dragon, but wards specifically designed against telepathic contact do;
- *Greater Restoration* can remove lingering psychic residue or a long-running control affliction after the connection is broken;
- *Mind Blank* represents the kind of total protection Tyren cannot penetrate.

The PCs’ anchored-mind resistance remains special and is not replaced by these tools.

## 5. The Missing Varen Volume

The stolen Marren volume is here.

Its surviving text finally identifies the being beneath Varen Keep:

> **TYREN — ANCIENT EMERALD DRAGON. SUBJECT’S PHYSICAL FORM REMAINS LIVING. PSYCHIC DISPERSAL MUST BE RECALLED BEFORE CONTAINMENT.**

A later passage describes the Radiant Cage as a unique ritual combining effects recognizable as *Imprisonment* and soul-binding magic.

The book confirms:

- Tyren’s body is beneath Varen Keep;
- the Radiant Well powers the Cage;
- the Unturned were required because ordinary ritual participants could not remain themselves near Tyren;
- breaking the Cage without first dealing with Tyren’s external network could release an ancient dragon with immediate access to dominated minds outside the prison.

This is the dragon reveal.

## 6. Why Tyren Has Not Simply Escaped

The research answers an important rules question.

Tyren can project thought through willing or compromised minds, but the Cage still binds **his body, soul, and permanent identity** to the lower chamber.

He can possess or dominate people temporarily.

He cannot permanently transfer himself into a new host while the Cage holds.

Perrin’s bargain did not release Tyren. It created a legal-sized crack in a prison designed to stop force.

## The Arcanist’s Fate

The arcanist can surrender, bargain, flee, or fight depending on the party’s approach.

The arcanist remains a one-chapter NPC unless the players choose to maintain the relationship.

## Consequences

The river begins clearing.

The party now knows the threat is not an abstract spirit.

There is an **ancient emerald dragon alive beneath Varen Keep**.

The next question is how far his network has already spread.

## Milestone

Advance the party to **level 9** after the Maren Isle crisis is resolved and the Varen research identifying Tyren as an imprisoned ancient emerald dragon is recovered.

## Kerren Settlement

![](img/adventure/NCFA/kerren_settlement.png)

Kerren is a thri-kreen settlement built among earthen mounds and underground chambers.

It is not a dungeon and its residents are not automatically hostile.

### Why It Matters

Kerren sits along a courier route that has recently changed behavior.

Couriers once traveled in small independent groups. Now different companies stop at exactly the same places, depart at the same times, and sometimes change direction simultaneously without exchanging messages.

Kerren residents have noticed because outsiders repeatedly cross their territory.

### Adventure

The party can earn cooperation through respectful contact, trade, translation magic, patient observation, or assistance with an unrelated local problem.

If trusted, Kerren provides:

- maps of courier movements;
- testimony that synchronized behavior extends beyond Bellweather;
- a captured courier who remembers receiving an “obvious” instruction without hearing a voice;
- evidence that the routes ultimately connect to Bellweather commerce.

Violence closes this information source but does not end the campaign.

### Reward

Regional intelligence, safe passage, supplies, and one useful consumable or crafted item.

---

## Waylen Camp

![](img/adventure/NCFA/waylen_camp.png)

Waylen Camp is a mobile mixed-species community of herders, hunters, guides, and traders.

### Why It Matters

The camp has watched troop and merchant movements for months.

Its guides can identify which groups changed from ordinary disciplined movement into something stranger: entire columns stopping, turning, or changing camp with no visible messenger.

### Adventure

The party can gain trust by sharing useful information, providing medical aid, protecting a caravan, or negotiating fairly for route access.

Waylen guides can lead the party around one hostile road entirely, reducing later combat.

They also provide a crucial strategic conclusion:

> The synchronized movement is spreading **outward from Bellweather**, not inward toward it.

That is the first clear sign that Bellweather is the source of the expansion rather than merely another target.

### Reward

Guides, mounts, route intelligence, and a standing trade relationship.

---

## Rellan Enclave

![](img/adventure/NCFA/rellan_enclave.png)

Rellan is a lizardfolk enclave built around flooded ruins and old stonework.

It is a community, not a monster lair.

### Why It Matters

Rellan oral tradition preserves memories that Bellweather’s written archives lost.

Their oldest stories describe a **green-glass dragon whose voice moved through other mouths** and a small group of people who could stand before it without bowing.

The story does not use the word Unturned, but the parallel is obvious.

### The Missing Ritual Detail

If the party earns access to the enclave’s old ritual tradition, they learn one fact absent from Marren’s surviving books:

> The Cage was designed so the Well could pull Tyren’s mind **inward**. Reversing the current would push his mind **outward**.

This is the conceptual key to Tyren’s final plan.

Rellan elders warn that anyone promising to “awaken the spirit beneath the city” is not opening a source of civic power. They are reversing a prison.

### Reward

Regional lore, safe passage, and one ritual component or charm useful in disrupting a Radiant Well node during Chapter 17.

---

## Emmer Hold

![](img/adventure/NCFA/emmer_hold.png)

Emmer Hold is a fortified kobold-majority mining and engineering community.

Most residents want trade and survival, not war.

A militant faction inside the hold has recently begun behaving with the same synchronized certainty seen at Paden Square.

### Why It Matters

This is Tyren’s first significant attempt to create a stable controlled group **outside Bellweather**.

The militants received weapons and promises through normal intermediaries. Then their decision-making changed.

### The Psychic Relay

The militant command room contains a fist-sized **emerald resonator** cut from a mineral formation connected to old Varen excavation material.

It is not a magic item intended for player use.

It functions as a psychic relay: a place where Tyren’s projected influence becomes easier to sustain.

Destroying or warding it causes several controlled militants to become confused, frightened, or immediately willing to stop fighting.

### Adventure Approaches

The party can:

- expose the militant leadership to the rest of the hold;
- infiltrate the command chamber;
- destroy the resonator;
- negotiate with residents who know their relatives are acting unlike themselves;
- fight the controlled command group while protecting civilians.

Run the hold as a living settlement with a militant faction rather than as an extermination dungeon.

### Evidence

The relay stores no written confession, but its shipping container came through one of the same commercial intermediary networks that appeared in Koven House records.

Combined with old Cardon-linked financing, this is another piece of the Perrin puzzle.

### Reward

If the hold is freed rather than conquered, Bellweather gains skilled engineers and a safer trade route.

# Confronting Perrin

The case can be built from several independent pieces of evidence; no single perfect proof is required.

By now the party can reasonably assemble several independent facts:

- Koven House financing touched Cardon-connected intermediaries;
- Perrin benefited politically from criminal coordination;
- Tyren requires a willing conduit to reach beyond the Radiant Cage;
- synchronized expansion originates in Bellweather;
- Perrin has repeatedly been near critical moments of unnatural obedience;
- Perrin’s rise has coincided with the growth of the network.

If the players reach a different theory first, let them investigate it. NPC exposition does not force suspicion of Perrin.

When they finally confront Perrin directly with Tyren, the Spirit, or the unexplained influence, Perrin has reached a point where he no longer wants to pretend the power itself is shameful.

## Perrin Tells the Truth

Perrin admits that a presence beneath Bellweather came to him before the campaign.

He believed it was the Spirit of the City.

It knew the city. It knew the Cardon family. It offered him the power he needed to restore both.

He admits using that power to influence people.

He admits organizing the criminal coalition to discredit the Council.

He admits that the Spirit began using the network in ways he did not order.

He does **not** describe himself as Tyren’s servant.

He believes he has been using a dangerous power for Bellweather’s benefit.

## The Party’s Strongest Argument

The party can tell Perrin that Tyren is an ancient emerald dragon imprisoned for trying to control the city fifty years ago.

Perrin does not have to deny the records anymore.

His answer should reveal how far he has moved:

> **“Then the old city was afraid of the same thing you’re afraid of now: a power that works.”**

The exact wording can vary; preserve the underlying idea.

Perrin now believes the moral question is not whether control is wrong in the abstract. He believes the question is **who uses it and for what purpose**.

## Can the Party Turn Perrin Here?

They can shake him.

They can make him doubt Tyren.

They can affect how he behaves later.

A single Persuasion check cannot erase years of choices or end the campaign.

Likewise, Perrin remains persuadable in principle; meaningful change requires accumulated evidence, relationship, and choices.

Record the quality of the confrontation:

**Cracked:** Perrin is visibly shaken and begins secretly considering a way out.

**Defiant:** Perrin believes the party is too frightened of power to understand what Bellweather needs.

**Hostile:** Perrin concludes the party will destroy everything he has built if he does not stop them.

This state affects Chapter 17.

## Perrin’s Core Refusal

Whatever his emotional state, Perrin does **not** surrender the power voluntarily here.

He believes giving it up would abandon Bellweather to the same indecision and weakness he has spent years overcoming.

The corruption arc is now explicit.

# Completing the Regional Arc

After the Perrin confrontation, let the party choose how to weaken Tyren before returning to the central political conflict.

A third regional success, destruction of the Emmer relay, a major alliance, or another equivalent player-driven achievement is enough to establish that Tyren’s outward network has been damaged.

Unused named locations remain in the world and can be visited later.

## Milestone

The party should leave the regional arc at **level 14**. The next level comes from surviving the Zennar betrayal.

### DM Truth: Perrin Decides the Party Is a Threat

After the confrontation, Perrin understands that the party knows too much.

If he is Cracked, he delays and searches for a solution that preserves Bellweather without giving up everything.

If Defiant or Hostile, he moves faster.

In every case, Tyren pushes one conclusion:

> The party will break the city unless Perrin removes them from the board.

That leads to Zennar.

# Chapter 16: Zennar Outpost

**Recommended level:** 14  
**Expected milestone:** 15  
**Likely table time:** 1–2 sessions

## Modern Running Sheet

| | |
|---|---|
| **Chapter purpose** | Make Perrin’s corruption undeniable: he uses civic authority to remove the people threatening his control. |
| **Primary objectives** | Detect the betrayal, survive or overturn the trap, preserve evidence, return to Bellweather. |
| **Player agency** | Inspect the packet, approach covertly, negotiate with the outpost, fake compliance, escape, expose the order, or fight a limited strike force. |

## Table-Ready Encounter Package

> Run Zennar as a political trap and survival chapter, not a fortress-clearing dungeon.
>
> **Quiet kill team:** 1 **Assassin**, 2 **Veterans**, 1 **Mage**, and 4 **Guards**. Use the Assassin only if the party reaches level 15 well equipped and rested; otherwise replace it with another Veteran.
>
> Attackers withdraw if the operation becomes public, loud, or clearly unsuccessful. Most of the outpost is not aware of the murder order.
>
> The strongest victory is evidence plus escape, not killing the entire garrison.

### Rewards and Treasure

Evidence is the main reward. If the party resolves the outpost diplomatically, use 1,000 gp-equivalent restitution, supplies, or a civic/diplomatic favor rather than a dungeon hoard.


### Stat Block Setup

| Role | 5etools lookup / setup |
|---|---|
| Kill-team leader | **Assassin** or **Veteran** |
| Professional soldier | **Veteran** |
| Commandant | **Knight** |
| Covert handler | **Spy** |
| Arcane support | **Mage** |
| Garrison | **Guard** |


## Why Perrin Sends Them

By level 14 the party has:

- identified Tyren as an ancient emerald dragon;
- learned the Radiant Cage exists;
- confronted Perrin about the bargain;
- damaged Tyren’s regional expansion;
- become the people most capable of exposing Perrin and stopping the Well from being reversed.

Perrin now believes the party threatens everything he has built.

Tyren does not need to dominate Perrin into betraying them.

Perrin makes the choice.

## The Official Mission

Perrin presents an urgent diplomatic/security assignment to **Zennar Outpost**.

The visible mission is plausible: establish whether the outpost will cooperate against regional instability and whether it can help isolate Varen Keep.

The party receives sealed credentials and instructions.

The packet contains a second order for the Zennar commandant.

### Perrin’s Betrayal by State

Use the Perrin state recorded after the party confronted him.

**Cracked Perrin:** the hidden order demands detention, disarmament, and removal from Bellweather’s affairs. It authorizes lethal force if the party resists. Perrin tells himself he is preventing a civil conflict, not ordering murder.

**Defiant Perrin:** the order describes the party as compromised by Tyren research and authorizes permanent removal if capture is impractical.

**Hostile Perrin:** the order is explicitly lethal and asks for proof of death.

All three versions are betrayals.

Cracked Perrin remains responsible even when his wording is softer.

## 1. The Road to Zennar

Give the party one quiet opportunity to inspect the packet before arrival.

Opening and resealing it should be possible with appropriate magic, tools, or care. If they refuse to tamper with diplomatic papers, later clues still expose the betrayal.

## 2. Approach

Zennar is a disciplined frontier outpost, not a dungeon full of enemies waiting for initiative.

A patrol escorts diplomatic visitors to a controlled reception area.

The soldiers are professional and do not all know about the hidden order.

## 3. Warning Signs

If the party has not opened the packet, give redundant clues:

- quarters chosen for containment rather than comfort;
- unusual interest in where the party stores weapons;
- civilians moved out of their corridor;
- a second courier arriving from Bellweather with updated instructions;
- guards who appear confused about whether the party is guest or prisoner.

Successful Insight or Investigation reveals the danger earlier. Failure never erases it.

## 4. The Commandant’s Choice

The commandant is not part of the recurring cast. Use a **Knight** or **Veteran** as needed.

The commandant may dislike Perrin’s instruction but still consider it legally valid.

The party can:

- expose contradictions in the order;
- present evidence of Tyren;
- convince the commandant Perrin is compromised;
- steal the outpost’s copy;
- bargain for release;
- fake compliance;
- escape.

A strong social solution should be as valid as combat.

## 5. Assassination or Arrest Attempt

If the betrayal reaches violence, use a compact strike force.

Suggested hard encounter for four level-14 PCs:

- 3 **Veterans**;
- 1 **Mage**;
- 4 **Guards** as support and pressure.

If the party is extremely optimized and fully rested, upgrade one Veteran to a **Knight** or equivalent. Keep most of the outpost outside initiative rather than treating the entire garrison as one encounter.

Attackers withdraw or surrender when the operation becomes publicly indefensible.

## 6. Evidence

The party can secure up to four forms of proof:

1. the original hidden order;
2. the commandant’s received copy;
3. testimony from personnel who were briefed on the operation;
4. a Bellweather courier record showing the order originated through Perrin’s office.

Two credible forms are enough to make denial politically difficult.

## Return to Bellweather

The city they return to should feel **better and worse at the same time**.

Crime is down.

Major streets are orderly.

Construction and trade are moving quickly.

Public disputes end unusually fast.

People praise Perrin for stabilizing the city while the party was away.

And something is wrong.

Groups of citizens repeat the same phrases. Workers change plans simultaneously. Clerks approve requests they had rejected minutes earlier. People who later come out of the effect cannot explain why a decision felt so obviously correct.

Bellweather is becoming Tyren’s successful prototype.

## Default Council Positions on Return

Adjust these according to actual play, but use them as a baseline if nothing else has changed.

**Wern Ursen** has been removed from direct command or politically sidelined after refusing to place the entire guard structure under Perrin’s unified emergency authority. Perrin uses Wern’s genuinely harsh earlier policies to make the removal look reasonable.

**Ulric Evern** remains institutionally active but has unexplained gaps in memory and has signed several decrees he does not remember drafting. Tyren can control Ulric because Ulric is not anchored like Brann or Brakkus. Ulric becomes powerful evidence that something supernatural is happening inside government.

**Bishop Brann** publicly refuses to endorse permanent emergency control. He cannot be mentally seized, so Perrin has to isolate, discredit, or politically work around him rather than simply switch him off.

**Brakkus Ledgerhelm** keeps Ledger House functioning as a refuge. He is not leading an armed rebellion. He simply refuses to hand guests over without lawful cause and cannot be compelled to change his mind supernaturally.

## No Cause for Alarm Beat

Notices appear throughout Bellweather:

> **CIVIC NOTICE:** Bellweather remains secure. Essential services are operating at record capacity. Reports of widespread coercion and administrative irregularity are being investigated. Residents are asked to avoid rumor and disruption. **There is no cause for alarm.**

At this point the phrase should be frightening precisely because much of the city really does look calm.

## Perrin’s Point of No Return

The Zennar order matters because Perrin is no longer using control only to solve civic problems.

He used the city’s institutions to protect **his own power** from people who could expose him.

Whatever good he still wants for Bellweather, the distinction between the city’s needs and Perrin’s needs has collapsed.

## Preparing for Varen

The party can return openly, gather Brann/Brakkus/Ulric/Wern as allies, use regional contacts, or move covertly.

Allies should reduce complications rather than join the dragon fight as DM-controlled heroes.

Examples:

- Brakkus keeps controlled civilians and guards from overwhelming the party’s escape route;
- Brann protects noncombatants and helps break a citywide influence pulse;
- Wern provides routes, timing, and military intelligence;
- Ulric exposes legal orders and removes bureaucratic barriers;
- regional allies cut off reinforcements or supply routes.

## Milestone

Advance the party to **level 15** when the Zennar betrayal is survived and credible evidence of Perrin’s order is preserved. The party reaches level 16 only after confronting controlled Bellweather and opening the route to Varen Keep.

# Chapter 17: Varen Keep and the Radiant Cage

**Recommended level:** 15–16  
**Campaign finale**  
**Likely table time:** 3–5 sessions including the controlled-Bellweather return

## Modern Running Sheet

| | |
|---|---|
| **Chapter purpose** | Stop Tyren’s attempt to reverse the Radiant Cage, confront Perrin’s choices, release and defeat the dragon, and decide Bellweather’s political and magical future. |
| **Primary objectives** | Reach the lower keep, disable the outward-control network, stop the Well reversal, survive Tyren’s release, defeat the ancient emerald dragon. |
| **Player agency** | Infiltration, coalition assault, secret routes, sabotage, negotiation with controlled defenders, or direct attack. Perrin’s final stance depends on earlier play. |
| **Fail forward** | Missing objectives makes the dragon fight harder; it never removes the dragon payoff or ends the campaign through one failed check. |
| **Final revelation** | The “Spirit of the City” is physically real, physically imprisoned, and has used Perrin as the door through which an old conquest could begin again. |

![](img/adventure/NCFA/varen_keep.png)

*Use the existing Varen Keep map. Run it as set pieces rather than a room-by-room clear.*

## Return to Controlled Bellweather

The party returns from Zennar at **level 15**. Bellweather is safer, cleaner, busier, and more efficient than they remember—and visibly less voluntary.

Crime is sharply reduced. Work crews move on schedule. Public projects advance. Residents repeat the same reassuring phrases, pause at the same moments, and sometimes obey minor instructions before realizing they were given.

Brakkus and Bishop Brann remain fully themselves. Jord and the PCs can feel Tyren's pressure but remain subject only to the established one-action *Command* ceiling.

Run this as a city-scale problem rather than a mandatory street-by-street clear. The party can expose Perrin's order, rally allies, sever relays, protect anchored people, infiltrate the civic control network, or create another credible route to Varen Keep.

**Milestone to 16:** Advance the party to **level 16** once they have materially broken or bypassed Bellweather's control network and opened a viable route to Varen Keep.

## Table-Ready Finale

> The final payoff is a **guaranteed dragon fight**. Earlier success changes Tyren's advantages; it does not remove the dragon from the campaign.
>
> **Stage 1 — Reach the lower keep:** 2–3 set pieces using **Veteran**, **Knight**, **Mage**, **Helmed Horror**, or controlled defenders appropriate to the allies and nodes still active.
>
> **Stage 2 — Break the nodes:** objective encounters, not room-by-room clearing.
>
> **Stage 3 — Perrin at the Well:** social/ritual confrontation with Perrin's state determined by earlier choices.
>
> **Stage 4 — Tyren released:** use the official **Ancient Emerald Dragon** stat block as the chassis, plus the Tyren overlay in the Custom Game Statistics appendix.

## The Situation

Tyren and Perrin are attempting to reverse the Radiant Well.

Perrin believes fully opening the Well will give him enough power to end Bellweather’s instability permanently. He now sees direct control as an efficient form of governance: crime can be stopped before it begins, corrupt officials can be made to act properly, and civic projects can proceed without obstruction.

Tyren wants something much larger.

The same reversed current that would let Perrin dominate Bellweather will let Tyren push his mind outward through every connected person, relay, trade route, and controlled node the network has established.

Bellweather is the first city.

Tyren intends it to be the first of many.

## Why the Dragon Fight Is Guaranteed

The party cannot solve the campaign by simply leaving Tyren sealed while breaking a few political connections.

The Radiant Cage and outward-control network have become entangled.

To stop Tyren from broadcasting through the Well, the party must disable the mechanisms reversing the current.

Doing so forces Tyren’s dispersed psychic presence back toward his physical body—the same principle used by the Unturned fifty years ago.

But fifty years of strain, Perrin’s willing conduit, and the reversed Well have damaged the original prison beyond repair.

When the party successfully stops the outward broadcast, the **Radiant Cage fails physically**.

Tyren is released.

Stopping the mind-control apocalypse causes the final dragon fight.

That is the price of ending the threat instead of postponing it.

# Finale Structure

Run Varen Keep as four major stages.

## Stage 1 — Reach the Lower Keep

Use 2–3 set pieces, not thirty rooms.

Existing map elements can include:

- outer watchposts;
- the thorn maze;
- barracks and controlled defenders;
- unstable teleporters;
- officer quarters;
- service tunnels discovered through earlier maps or allies.

### Defenders

Most defenders are not fanatics. Some are controlled guards, workers, criminals, or mercenaries.

Use **Veterans**, **Knights**, **Guards**, **Mages**, and other appropriate official stat blocks in small groups.

Characters who use mind wards, *Dispel Magic*, *Greater Restoration* after severing a connection, or nonlethal tactics can free or bypass some defenders.

The objectives allow the party to bypass, free, or contain controlled Bellweather citizens instead of requiring lethal force.

### Ally Benefits

For each major ally or regional success earned before the finale, remove one complication:

- no exterior reinforcement wave;
- one watchpost already neutralized;
- a service route is open;
- controlled civilians have been evacuated;
- one defensive ward is already disabled.

Allied NPCs remain outside the main dragon initiative unless the players explicitly engineer their direct participation.

---

## Stage 2 — Break the Three Nodes

The Radiant Cage complex contains three systems that Tyren and Perrin are using to reverse the original ritual.

The party can tackle them in any order.

### Node A: The Chorus Chamber

This chamber maintains Tyren’s active connection to controlled minds outside the Keep.

It contains resonant emerald material similar to the device at Emmer Hold.

**Objective:** destroy, disrupt, or ward the resonance array.

Possible methods:

- physical destruction;
- Arcana or Religion challenge while under attack;
- using the Rellan ritual component;
- overloading the array with psychic/radiant energy;
- a creative equivalent.

**Benefit if disabled:**

- controlled reinforcements outside the Keep lose coordination;
- Tyren cannot use the campaign-specific **Seize the Anchored** effect during the dragon fight;
- when Tyren dies, he has no prepared network into which his consciousness can easily escape.

This is the most important node for a clean victory.

### Node B: The Civic Relay

Perrin has built an administrative-magical network around the Well: orders, signal crystals, schedules, city maps, and lists of officials and institutions.

This is the machinery of Bellweather being treated like one body.

**Objective:** sever Perrin’s ability to direct the network.

Possible methods:

- destroy the central relay;
- replace the command sequence with a shutdown order;
- use Ulric’s official seals to invalidate linked civic instructions;
- use Wern’s military codes to break guard synchronization;
- persuade Perrin to deactivate it.

**Benefit if disabled:**

- no controlled guard/minion support during the final fight;
- Bellweather begins waking up before Tyren is dead;
- Perrin loses the immediate proof that “control works.”

### Node C: The Well Reversal

This mechanism is physically reversing the Radiant Well’s original inward pull.

**Objective:** stop the outward flow.

Possible methods:

- stabilize and reverse arcane regulators;
- break the control lattice;
- complete part of the old Unturned rite;
- force the system into shutdown.

**Benefit if disabled:**

- Tyren cannot project his will citywide during the final confrontation;
- the global-scale plan ends;
- the process of shutting it down begins the unavoidable physical collapse of the Radiant Cage.

### Running the Nodes

Each node should be an objective encounter with defenders and environmental pressure rather than a single Arcana roll.

A good default is **3 successes before 2 complications** using different plausible actions while a small elite force interferes.

Each node accepts multiple approaches; no single skill is required.

# Stage 3 — Perrin at the Well

Perrin is present in the lower complex when the final node is threatened.

This is not a mandatory boss fight against Perrin.

His behavior depends on the state recorded during the level-14 confrontation and what the party has done since.

## Cracked Perrin

Perrin has been trying not to admit that the party was right.

Seeing the physical containment machinery and the Well destabilizing can finally break the story he has told himself about the Spirit.

He may help disable one node, order controlled defenders to stand down, or give the party a crucial piece of access.

He still remains responsible for everything he chose before this moment.

## Defiant Perrin

Perrin believes the party is about to destroy the only power capable of permanently fixing Bellweather.

He tries to stop them through guards, locks, civic authority, and the relay system.

He may still hesitate when Tyren begins speaking openly about control beyond Bellweather.

## Hostile Perrin

Perrin has fused his own political survival with Bellweather’s future.

He actively attempts to keep the Well reversal running and treats the party as enemies of the city.

Use an appropriate non-boss NPC chassis if Perrin must enter combat, but keep his real power in command, access, and controlled defenders rather than suddenly turning him into a high-level wizard.

## The Truth Perrin Cannot Rationalize

As the final node fails, Tyren no longer needs to maintain the fiction of being Bellweather’s benevolent spirit.

Perrin experiences the dragon’s full presence for the first time.

The party should not need a speech to understand what happened.

The chamber shakes.

Emerald light moves beneath the stone.

The enormous restraints below begin to break.

Whatever Perrin thought he made a bargain with, it has claws, wings, and a body large enough to have required a fortress to bury.

Perrin’s reaction belongs to the campaign state:

- horror;
- denial;
- rage at Tyren;
- desperate insistence that the party can still control the situation;
- or a final attempt to help.

Tyren’s deception does not erase Perrin’s agency. Perrin was deceived about what Tyren was, but Perrin knowingly chose manipulation, criminal conspiracy, direct control, and betrayal.

# The Release

When the Well Reversal is stopped, the Radiant Cage performs one final version of its original purpose: it yanks Tyren’s projected consciousness back toward his own body.

The damaged prison cannot survive the return.

The lower chamber opens.

Tyren is released.

This is the campaign’s dragon payoff.

# Stage 4 — Tyren, Ancient Emerald Dragon

Use the official **Ancient Emerald Dragon** stat block from *Fizban’s Treasury of Dragons* as Tyren’s base statistics.

Use Tyren’s core ancient emerald dragon stat block at full strength. This is the final fight of a level-3-to-16 campaign and should demand major resources.

Balance the encounter through the objectives the party completed, not by quietly reducing Tyren’s identity.

## Default Final Fight

If the party disabled **two or more nodes**, fight Tyren as the full official Ancient Emerald Dragon without an additional minion wave.

Use Varen’s lower chamber and Well as meaningful terrain.

Allow the party the benefit of every defensive preparation they earned.

## If the Chorus Chamber Still Functions

Tyren retains a campaign-specific legendary/lair option:

### Seize the Anchored

Tyren targets one anchored PC or Jord he can perceive. The target makes the appropriate Wisdom save.

On a failure, Tyren imposes one Command-like imperative on the target’s next turn, following the campaign’s hard ceiling:

- one compelled turn/action only;
- no forced attack on an ally;
- no expenditure of a major limited resource;
- no irreversible roleplay decision;
- no suicidal order.

A **Ring of Mind Shielding** makes the wearer immune.

Brakkus and Bishop Brann are immune if they are somehow present.

If the Chorus Chamber was disabled, Tyren **cannot use this ability**.

## If the Civic Relay Still Functions

Add one limited support complication rather than a full army:

- 2 controlled **Veterans** arrive at the start of round 2; or
- one controlled **Mage** supports from a balcony until freed/defeated; or
- a civic defense mechanism creates cover/hazard for Tyren.

Only one of the three effects is active at a time.

If the Civic Relay was disabled, Tyren fights without controlled Bellweather support.

## If the Well Reversal Was Only Partially Stabilized

Use one additional environmental effect at initiative count 20:

- psychic shockwave causing forced movement;
- radiant eruption from a marked zone;
- temporary difficult terrain as gravity/force bends toward the Well.

A completely stabilized Well removes this extra effect.

## Tyren’s Tactics

Tyren has spent fifty years thinking about control.

He should fight intelligently.

He uses mobility, psychic abilities, breath weapon, terrain, and isolation rather than standing in melee until dead.

He recognizes the Ring of Mind Shielding as a threat if its immunity becomes obvious.

He understands Brakkus and Brann are the same kind of problem the Unturned were.

He does not waste turns repeatedly attempting effects on creatures he knows are immune.

## No Forced PvP

Even in the final fight, Tyren’s anchored-mind control does not force a PC to attack another PC.

Keep the premise consistent all the way to the end.

# Tyren at 0 Hit Points

The campaign established that killing a host was not automatically enough to kill Tyren.

The final objectives determine whether that remains a problem.

## If the Chorus Chamber Was Disabled

Tyren’s external network has collapsed.

When his physical body dies, there is no prepared web of dominated minds for his consciousness to inhabit.

The Radiant Well pulls the remaining psychic presence inward and it dies with the dragon.

The threat ends.

## If the Chorus Chamber Still Functions

Tyren attempts to escape through the remaining network.

Run a short final objective challenge: **4 successes before 3 complications**.

Valid actions include:

- destroying the Chorus system now;
- using the Radiant Well to pull the escaping mind inward;
- using the Ring of Mind Shielding as a protected contact point while manipulating the network;
- Arcana or Religion to complete the old containment sequence;
- *Dispel Magic*, *Greater Restoration*, or another relevant high-level spell used creatively;
- severing controlled NPCs from the relay;
- another plausible player plan.

Failure should create a costly complication, not erase the campaign victory outright. Tyren may leave behind a fragment, scar, or future threat, but he does not simply possess a PC and declare the campaign lost.

# Perrin After Tyren

Separating or killing Tyren does not automatically absolve Perrin.

The relevant facts remain:

- Perrin willingly accepted supernatural influence;
- he used it to manipulate people;
- he organized criminals to discredit the Council;
- he concealed Tyren’s independent control after discovering it;
- he knowingly chose direct mental control at Storen Gate;
- he continued after learning Tyren was the same force that nearly destroyed old Bellweather;
- he used civic authority against the party at Zennar;
- he attempted to make control permanent.

Perrin was deceived about Tyren’s identity.

Perrin was also corrupted through his own choices.

Let the players and surviving Bellweather institutions decide what happens next.

Possible outcomes include trial, imprisonment, exile, political collapse, cooperation in repairing the damage, or another consequence earned through play.

Perrin’s ultimate fate is left to the players and the world; neither redemption nor execution is predetermined.

---

# The Radiant Well

After Tyren is defeated, the Well remains.

The party and Bellweather must decide what to do with a source of power capable of both containment and mass psychic amplification.

Possible choices include:

- seal access;
- destroy the control infrastructure while preserving the natural/magical Well;
- place it under shared custody among multiple institutions;
- study it under strict safeguards;
- collapse Varen’s lower chamber;
- another player-created solution.

No single Council member automatically gets control.

Brakkus’s only strong position is that no one person should quietly control something capable of taking away another person’s will.

Brann emphasizes consent and the moral danger of “good” coercion.

Ulric wants transparent legal custody and records.

Wern wants to know how the city defends the site from whoever comes next.

# Epilogue: No Cause for Alarm

A public celebration belongs in the epilogue only if it matches the table’s outcome.

Show concrete consequences:

- people waking from control and learning what they did;
- businesses deciding whether Perrin-era agreements still stand;
- guards confronting orders they carried out under influence;
- families returning to normal routines;
- political arguments happening openly again;
- Ledger House full of people disagreeing loudly because they are finally allowed to.

The hopeful ending is not that Bellweather becomes calm.

It is that Bellweather becomes capable of tolerating disagreement without treating disagreement as failure.

If the title gets one final spoken callback, earn it from the actual ending.

A surviving official might begin another reassuring notice and stop before writing the familiar phrase.

Brakkus might be asked whether the city is safe now and answer with something dry rather than certainty.

The final title phrase is optional and follows the actual outcome.

The campaign has already taught the table what they mean.

# Appendix B: Tyren Control Quick Rules

## Ordinary NPC — Early

Use **Suggestion** as the rules model.

- Wisdom save.
- Charmed on failure.
- Instruction must be achievable.
- No obviously self-destructive command.
- Damage or appropriate magic can end the effect as normal for the model.

## Ordinary NPC — Group

Use **Mass Suggestion** as the rules model.

Use this when a scene requires a coordinated group, not as a casual background power.

## Ordinary NPC — Late Direct Control

Use **Dominate Person** as the rules model.

This is for late-campaign ordinary NPCs only.

Dominate Person-style control never applies to the PCs or Jord.

## PCs and Jord

Use the campaign’s **Command ceiling**.

- Wisdom saving throw.
- One turn / one compelled action on failure.
- Full control returns immediately.
- No forced attacks on allies.
- No forced major resource expenditure.
- No irreversible roleplay choices.
- No suicidal commands.

## Brakkus and Bishop Brann

Direct Tyren control fails.

No save is needed when the only effect is Tyren attempting to seize their actions.

They remain vulnerable to ordinary persuasion, deception, threats, environmental danger, and unrelated magic.

## Ring of Mind Shielding

A wearer is immune to Tyren’s control because the ring prevents the unwanted telepathic connection Tyren needs to establish.

This is a campaign-specific interaction with Tyren, not a blanket rewrite of the item.

# Appendix I: Session Prep Template for This Version

This campaign is designed so the DM does not need to write an entire session from scratch every week.

For each session, prepare only the following.

## 1. What Can Change Tonight?

One sentence.

Examples:

- The party can prove Koven House is the Covenant hub.
- The party can learn Tyren controlled Old Bellweather.
- Perrin can cross the line into direct control.

## 2. Three Scenes

**Arrival/choice.** Present the problem and at least two approaches.

**Pressure.** Combat, investigation, social conflict, hazard, or time pressure complicates the plan.

**Decision.** End around a choice, objective, or confrontation that changes the situation.

## 3. Essential Information

Write the clues the party must be able to obtain.

Never attach a campaign-critical clue to one pass/fail roll.

## 4. Opposition

For each active group, write:

- what it wants;
- what it is doing now;
- what makes it retreat, surrender, bargain, or change plans.

## 5. Perrin/Tyren Movement

Ask one question:

**Did anything happen tonight that should change Perrin’s corruption or Tyren’s reach?**

If no, there is no forced Perrin/Tyren movement that session.

Not every session needs a main-plot reveal.

## 6. Return to Ledger House

Prepare:

- one visible city change;
- one familiar face;
- one consequence;
- one live opportunity.

That is enough.

# Appendix J: Campaign Constants

These are fixed facts of the campaign.

- **Independent world.** Not every antagonist, crime, monster, or tragedy connects to Tyren.
- **Bishop Brann.** Brann is not secretly evil.
- **Wern Ursen.** Wern is not secretly evil or a hidden Tyren loyalist; his hard security choices stand on their own.
- **Played history.** The Slums and Blackstone remain what actually happened and are not retroactively Perrin plots.
- **Anchored minds.** PCs and Jord can never be controlled beyond one single *Command*-style action.
- **Player agency.** Mind control never decides a character’s beliefs, roleplay, or irreversible choices.
- **Iron Covenant.** The Covenant is a criminal organization Perrin helped organize; it is not a Tyren-worshiping cult.
- **Resistance.** An anchored sense of self creates resistance. Trauma is one possible cause of that anchoring, not a magical reward for suffering.
- **Perrin’s responsibility.** Tyren lied about being the Spirit of the City, but Perrin remains responsible for the choices he knowingly made.
- **Finale.** Tyren is physically released and fought as an ancient emerald dragon.
- **Level range.** The complete campaign runs from level 3 through level 16.
- **Structure.** Named locations remain real without becoming a mandatory reclamation checklist.

# Appendix L: Custom Game Statistics and Overlays

These entries modify or wrap official stat blocks already available in 5etools. They intentionally avoid recreating published monster text.

## Tyren — Ancient Emerald Dragon Overlay

**Base stat block:** **Ancient Emerald Dragon**.

Use the official block unchanged except for the campaign-specific additions below.

### Anchored Mind Rule

Tyren's ordinary control effects cannot hold an anchored mind.

- **Brakkus Ledgerhelm and Bishop Brann:** immune to Tyren's direct supernatural influence and control.
- **Player characters and Jord:** resistant. If Tyren attempts direct control, call for the appropriate Wisdom saving throw. On a failure, Tyren may compel **one single action** modeled on *Command*. Control ends immediately after that action.
- **Ring of Mind Shielding:** the wearer is immune to Tyren's influence and control because the ring blocks the telepathic connection Tyren requires.
- This rule never allows Tyren to force a PC or Jord into multi-round control, forced roleplay, permanent belief changes, or ongoing domination.

### Voice Through the Chorus

When a Radiant Cage node or psychic relay is active, Tyren can issue a telepathic imperative to creatures connected to his network.

For **ordinary NPCs**, model the effect with the closest appropriate official spell for the story stage: *Suggestion*, *Mass Suggestion*, *Geas*, or *Dominate Person*.

For **anchored minds**, the effect collapses to the one-action *Command*-style ceiling above.

### Seize the Anchored

When the finale needs direct pressure, Tyren may target one anchored creature he can perceive within the range you are already using for the official dragon's psychic abilities.

The target makes a Wisdom saving throw against Tyren's normal save DC.

On a failure, choose one simple imperative such as **Approach, Drop, Flee, Grovel, Halt, Move, Release, or Protect**. On that creature's next turn, the imperative consumes no more than one action or equivalent brief compelled behavior. Control then ends.

Direct attacks on another PC occur only if that player has explicitly welcomed that kind of scene. The default is movement, dropping/handing over an object, opening/closing something, standing aside, or losing one action.

### Node Advantages

Each active node in Varen Keep grants Tyren one campaign advantage.

**Chorus Chamber active:** once per round, Tyren can use Seize the Anchored without spending a legendary-action equivalent. Ordinary dominated defenders also remain coordinated.

**Civic Relay active:** controlled defenders gain advantage on their first saving throw each round and can reposition as a coordinated reaction when Tyren moves.

**Well Reversal active:** one lair/environmental effect tied to the Radiant Well remains available each round.

Disable a node and remove its advantage immediately.

## Perrin Cardon Overlay

### Normal Perrin

Use **Noble** as the baseline if statistics are needed. Perrin is not designed as a frontline combat encounter.

### Borrowed Influence

While Tyren's connection is subtle, Perrin can create an effect modeled on *Suggestion* against an ordinary NPC who can perceive him. Use a Wisdom save appropriate to the current tier; DC 14–16 is sufficient early.

This is supernatural influence, not ordinary Persuasion.

### Mass Influence

Late in the campaign, when Tyren is actively channeling through Perrin, model Perrin's influence over ordinary crowds using *Mass Suggestion*.

Mass Influence cannot target PCs, Jord, Brakkus, or Bishop Brann.

### Borrowed Command

When Tyren is actively present, Perrin can serve as the point of origin for Tyren's one-action effect against an anchored mind.

This follows the **Anchored Mind Rule** exactly. Perrin never gets a stronger control effect over a PC or Jord.

### Combat Use

If Perrin becomes physically hostile, he should rely on guards, positioning, social leverage, and Tyren's borrowed abilities. Perrin remains a social/political antagonist rather than becoming a high-CR martial boss.

## Brakkus Ledgerhelm

Use the existing authoritative **Brakkus Ledgerhelm** custom stat block from *No Cause for Alarm: Ruins of Bellweather*.

For this campaign add:

**Anchored Immunity.** Tyren cannot magically influence, charm, command, dominate, possess, or otherwise take direct control of Brakkus. This is the combined result of Brakkus's divine aura/protection and a deeply anchored identity shaped by trauma. It does not make Brakkus immune to lies, ordinary Persuasion, political pressure, or emotional manipulation.

## Bishop Brann

**Base stat block:** **Priest** unless a stronger priestly block is clearly needed for a late-tier defensive scene.

**Anchored Immunity.** Tyren cannot magically influence, charm, command, dominate, possess, or otherwise take direct control of Brann.

Brann's anchor is his devotion reinforced by childhood trauma: he watched his sister murdered by a gang and built his adult identity around refusing to let violence, fear, or hatred dictate who he becomes.

The gang that murdered Brann’s sister is unrelated to the Iron Covenant and Tyren.

## Jord

**Base stat block:** use the established Jord statistics if available; otherwise **Priest** is sufficient for NPC combat.

**Anchored Resistance.** Jord is resistant, not immune. Tyren can never do more than the one-action *Command*-style effect on him.

Jord's anchor is intentionally a little funny: he lives for a good deal so completely that some part of his mind interrogates every proposition for the catch. Tyren can force through for a moment; he cannot keep Jord's mind.

## Wern Ursen

Use **Veteran** for most scenes or **Knight** when Wern needs to function as a serious armored commander. Wern is an elf and a career soldier. He is not secretly controlled by Tyren by default.

## Ulric Evern

Use **Noble** if statistics are required. Ulric's power is procedural and institutional, not combat-focused.

# Appendix N: Table-Use Chapter Checklist

Before running any chapter, verify:

1. **Level and milestone** match the campaign table.
2. **Map** is visible or the scene is intentionally theater-of-the-mind.
3. **Encounter roster** is distributed across scenes rather than stacked into one initiative.
4. **Stat blocks** are loaded in 5etools before play.
5. **Essential clues** cannot disappear behind one failed roll.
6. **Opposition morale** is defined.
7. **Rewards and Treasure** is prepared.
8. **Perrin/Tyren movement** is known behind the screen.
9. **Pepper continuity** is updated if still relevant.
10. **Return to Ledger House** includes one visible Bellweather change, one consequence, and one live opportunity.


# Final DM Summary

If the campaign ever feels complicated, return to this:

> **Perrin sincerely wants Bellweather restored. Tyren pretends to be the Spirit of the City and gives him influence. Perrin uses it for good, then for manipulation, then to organize criminals against the Council. Tyren hijacks that criminal network and learns to control groups. Perrin repeatedly discovers the danger and keeps the power because the results work. Eventually he chooses direct control himself. Tyren is actually an ancient emerald dragon trapped beneath Varen Keep, and Bellweather is his experiment for controlling every mind in the world. The party uncovers the truth, breaks the network, stops the Radiant Well from broadcasting Tyren’s will, releases the dragon in the process, and fights him at level 16.**

Everything else is an adventure that happens around that story.
